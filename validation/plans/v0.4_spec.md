## 1. GATE 4 measures the response with the claim's own statistic, in one group

**Evidence.** R1's Xist claim was called "metric invalid" by GATE 4, falsely (D1). The module probe
raised Xist 2-fold in every cell and measured the metric over all cells. For a mean over cells
that response is, by identity, the share of cells with the marker times the response in them:
+0.163 over all cells against delta_min 0.25, +0.631 over the 25.8% of B2's cells with Xist; with
`anchors.py`'s constants the probe needed at least 0.25 / 0.631 = 39.6% of cells with Xist
(`validation/exploratory/gate4_module_analytic.log`; Review 1 B8 and B10). GATE 4's interval is a
Monte Carlo interval over 200 injections into one dataset, not an uncertainty over donors
(Review 1 B9).

**Change.** GATE 4 injects the signal into one group and measures the response with the claim's
own statistic: the replicate-level difference between the groups that the effect test uses
(`effect._rep_values`, `effect._infer`), injected against its reference. Its interval is taken over
the replicates (the injection repeated within each replicate), so that it is an uncertainty over
donors. The response is judged against the direction the injection itself must move that
difference (an injection into the second group moves it one way, into the first the other), not
against the claim's direction.

**Decided.** The signal goes into the group where the probed genes (the module's, or the pair's
two) have more counts per cell in the data, not into the claimed group (2026-10-12). The rule must
not depend on the claim's wording: "Xist is higher in females" and "Xist is lower in males" are one
claim, but in the second the claimed group is the males. The present all-cell response stays in
the report as a diagnostic (2026-10-11).

**Decided** (2026-10-12) how the groups are compared: by the mean over each group's replicates of
the probed genes' counts per cell (each donor weighted equally, as in the effect test); on an exact
tie the probe runs in each group and the weaker response is judged. Every random draw of the
engine (GATE 4's injections, GATE 2's thinning) is keyed by the data and the claim's canonical
form: the metric, the genes, the groups' labels sorted, and the direction stated for that order. A
mirrored wording has the same canonical form, so it gets the same draws and the same verdict; two
different claims on the same data get different draws, so that their Monte Carlo errors are
independent, which a key of the data and the labels alone would not give.

**v2 test.** Valid mean-expression metrics of markers present in only some cells (10–40%) and of
markers present in all cells of one group, with the truth about validity from a mechanism other
than GATE 4's: a false "metric invalid" on them at most at its nominal; the module probe on valid
metrics, not only on a useless one (in v1 it ran only on N6c). Every claim also in its mirrored
wording (the order of the groups and the direction swapped), for the module and the coupling probe:
the verdict and its cause must be the same.

## 2. A module probe that creates no response by losing counts

**Evidence.** The module probe's sham thins every gene, the module's too, while the injection keeps
the module's counts. At a depth of 2,000 counts a marker of single counts gives a response of
0.360, of which the sham's loss of the single counts makes 0.178 and the 2-fold change 0.182
(`gate4_module_analytic.log`): on shallow data the probe passes for the wrong reason. With fewer
counts the response exceeds the identity's bound and even ln 2 (0.652 against 0.619 in the
marker's cells at a CP10k of 6; 1.20 for single counts at a depth of 2,000).

**Change.** The probe changes the module in the group where it has more counts per cell (change 1),
by thinning the module's genes there (a `fold`-times decrease, the counts staying real), against
the untouched data and without a sham: the loss of counts is then the signal itself, as it is for a
real decrease of expression, and the depth changes only by the module's share of the counts, which
is reported. GATE 4 checks the response against the direction of the injection itself (a
decrease must lower the metric in that group), not against the claim's direction. The coupling
probe keeps its sham: its injection and sham thin each gene with the same marginal keep
probability.

**Decided.** The owner found the flaw of this draft's first version: thinning the module "in the
comparison group" would, for Xist, thin it in the males, who have none, so the response would be
zero and R1 would fail again. Hence the group where the module is expressed, and since 2026-10-12,
where it is expressed in both, the group where it has more counts per cell, whatever the claim's
wording (change 1). Thinning without a sham; a decrease by default.

**v2 test.** Markers of single counts at depths from 1,000 to 20,000 counts per cell, valid and
blind by the independent truth: GATE 4's response must not exceed the response of the composition
change alone (computed by the oracle on the counts before thinning), and a blind metric must not
pass at a low depth. Among them a marker absent in one of the groups (as Xist in males and the Y
genes in females), each in both wordings ("higher in females", "lower in males"): the verdicts
must coincide.

## 3. The probe's strength tied to the SESOI

**Evidence.** In v1 GATE 4's dose and delta_min (0.5 × SESOI) came from the pilot, that is, from the
oracle (the pilot's saturation dose; README, limitations). A user has no pilot. The module probe's
default (2-fold in 30% of cells) cannot pass a well-detected mean-expression metric at a SESOI of
0.5: at most 0.3 × ln 2 = 0.208 against delta_min 0.25, by arithmetic (`gate4_module_dilution.log`).

**Change.** The probe's size is defined on the construct's scale and equals the claim's SESOI, so
that GATE 4 asks whether the metric shows at least half (delta_min) of a change that matters:
- module: fold = exp(SESOI) for a SESOI on the log scale (change 2);
- coupling: the strength at which the injected shared factor raises the correlation of the two
  genes' log expected expression by the SESOI, given their biological variances estimated from
  the data with the counting noise removed (a calculation on the data, not a pilot).

**Decided.** The dose computed from the data, and the report always gives the dose–response curve
next to the judgment. Before the freeze, a development check on v1's data (development data now):
the computed dose against the oracle's true change of the construct, at the three expression
levels.

**The code (v0.4.0.dev0).** `prereg["sesoi"]` is on the construct's scale by default
(`sesoi_scale="construct"`): for a coupling probe a change of the correlation of the pair's log
expected expression, in (0, 1); for a module probe a change of the natural-log expression, the probe
lowering the module exp(SESOI)-fold (`injected_signal.at_construct_change`). The coupling's strength
comes from the two genes' biological variances in the probed cells, by a Poisson-lognormal method of
moments floored at 0.01 (`biological_log_variance`, `coupling_strength_for`): the shared factor adds
v = Var(log sigmoid(s z)) to both genes' variances and to their covariance, the sham to the variances
only, so the correlation rises by v / sqrt((var_a + v)(var_b + v)) whatever the genes' own
correlation. GATE 4 injects within each replicate of the group with more counts (change 1), 50 times
each, and gives s_m, the mean of the replicates' responses with its 95% interval over the replicates
(a cluster-robust mean, `combined.one_group_mean`): PASS where the lower bound lies above zero in the
injection's direction, FAIL (blind) where the upper bound lies at or below zero, UNTESTED otherwise.
delta_min is recorded, not used. The detail gives the dose–response at 0.5×, 1× and 2× the SESOI (20
injections per replicate at the extra doses). s_m is the SESOI in the metric's units for GATE 0's
tolerance, the TOST and EFFECT BELOW SESOI; a SESOI declared on the metric's scale
(`sesoi_scale="observed"`) is used as declared; a SESOI on the construct's scale that no probe
translates is not used (SESOI_NOT_TRANSLATED), and the floor stands in (change 4). Not yet done: the
development check of the computed dose against the oracle's true change of the construct on v1's
data (decided above, before the freeze).

## 4. A rule for the SESOI without the oracle

**Evidence.** v1's SESOI per background and level was the pilot's: the smallest value of a grid
(0.005–1.0) at which the oracle reaches a correct definite outcome (NO DETECTABLE EFFECT by the TOST,
or NOT SUPPORTED against the direction) on at least 95% of the pilot's datasets of the background's
null (N1 on B1, N7 on B2; v1.md section 3.2). It needs only the spread of the metric over
replicates, which the user's own data give.

**Change.** The user declares the SESOI. The engine reports the design's floor: the smallest SESOI
at which this design confirms absence by the TOST with a power fixed in advance (v1's rule used
0.95), from the replicate-level spread of the metric within the groups and the replicates per
group. A declared SESOI below the floor is flagged (absence cannot be confirmed).

**Decided.** Without a declared SESOI the floor is used, and the report says in so many words:
"absence is confirmed only for effects of at least X". The dose follows from the SESOI (change 3).

**The pilot (2026-10-16).** The floor is in the metric's units, as every threshold (change 3, "a
SESOI in other units"). The pilot's second run computes it by hand on preprint section 4's setup,
SE × (t₀.₉₅,df + t₀.₉₇₅,df) from the replicate-level difference over the mice, and runs
`mi_3bin`'s claim at the floor next to the documented 0.1 (`real_pilot_plan.md`, "Deviation: a
second run of cases 1 and 2").

**Decided (2026-10-17).** Its final form with change 3's, after the diagnostics.

**Proposed (to be decided).** As in change 3's proposal: the floor in the metric's units, compared
with s_m.

**Decided (2026-10-17).** With change 3's architecture; the final wording after the second
diagnostics.

**Decided (2026-10-18).** With change 3's.

**The code (v0.4.0.dev0).** The floor is se × (t(1 − α, df) + t(0.975, df)) of the replicate-level
estimate (`effect.design_floor`, power 0.95 at a true difference of 0, v1's rule), in the metric's
units. A SESOI in the metric's units below it is flagged SESOI_BELOW_FLOOR. Without a SESOI the floor
is the margin of absence, flagged NO_SESOI_FLOOR, and the report says "absence is confirmed only for
effects of at least X"; EFFECT BELOW SESOI and the TOST that explains a difference by depth need a
pre-registered SESOI.

**v2 test.** Real effects and nulls run with the floor as the SESOI and with a SESOI declared below
it; the rates of NO DETECTABLE EFFECT on effects of the size of the floor and the flags, per level.

## 6. GATE 2: a correction can only remove a conclusion, never create one

**Evidence** (the decisive experiment and the reports of v1, after the results;
`v1_analyzers.log` section 5, `v1_correction_rule.log`). v0.3's correction for a composition
estimand thins the deeper group by one ratio, which is exact for a difference of capture. Where the
groups differ otherwise, it makes false effects: all 7 false SUPPORTED of N3 at f = 0.4 on the high
level had raw differences that the replicate-level test did not detect (p 0.06 to 0.34), which the
correction enlarged 1.36 to 2.69 times; a test over donors without the correction gives none of
the 83.

**Change.** SUPPORTED needs the effect detected, in one direction, by both the raw replicate-level
test (the same data without the correction, which the engine already runs) and the corrected one;
otherwise the verdict is INCONCLUSIVE. A correction can still remove a conclusion ("explained by
depth"), as now.

**Decided.** This principle, not a test of fit: the check of the data against the capture model
goes into the report, not into a gate. No separate correction for entry-level dropout.

**What the rule changes on v1's reports** (post hoc, `v1_correction_rule.log`): the false SUPPORTED
on null data fall from 54 to 36, all of them on cards at risk (N3 at f = 0.4: 7 to 0; S1's cells N1
5 to 4, N2 6 to 3, N5 8 to 8, N8 11 to 8); the true SUPPORTED on the real effects from 275 to 272
(E1 1, E2 2), none of the 118 valid cards with |Δ\*| ≥ SESOI. Of the 18 false SUPPORTED it removes,
the correction had enlarged the undetected raw difference on 15, shrunk it on 1 and reversed its
sign on 2 (both N2): v0.3 makes a sign reversed by the correction INCONCLUSIVE only where the raw
difference was detected, and the rule closes that gap too.

**Decided on absence (2026-10-12).** In v0.4 the rule does not bind a claim of absence. The engine
also runs the TOST on the uncorrected data, and a NO DETECTABLE EFFECT that only the corrected data
show is marked in the report: "absence shown only after the depth correction". On v1 the correction
made 85 of the 1,676 NO DETECTABLE EFFECT, where the same test on the uncorrected data was not
equivalent, and none of the 85 is wrong by v1's rules (`v1_correction_rule.log`, section 3). If v2
finds errors among such absences, v0.5 extends the rule to them.

**v2 test.** N3 at several dropout fractions, sized on the cards at risk, with a cell for the
SUPPORTED the rule removes; E2 and E3 at several capture ratios, to count the real effects it costs.
Real effects with an artifact outside the correction's family (E + N3, E + N8), with a cell for the
NO DETECTABLE EFFECT the correction made: its errors decide v0.5.

## 7. Tissue and cell-type composition

**Evidence.** v1 used one cell type per background (oligodendrocytes in B1, beta cells in B2), so it
did not test a claim whose groups differ in the shares of cell types or tissues. Claims about
ageing compare tissues or mixtures whose composition changes. v0.3 stratifies only by the factors a
user declares (`within`) and does not check composition.

**The pilot** (2026-10-16; `real_pilot_2.log`, claim 1b, on preprint section 4's setup). The
`norm_pearson` of Smad3 and Col1a1 rose from young to old, against the claimed decrease, with every
gate passing and the effect detected by the raw and the corrected test alike (first minus second
−0.3687 and −0.3664). The rise is largest in the females, means −0.043 against 0.608 (p 0.0893),
whose old group is the mammary gland only (728 cells) against 22 tissues in the young
(`real_pilot_2_select.log`); in the males, with 16 to 20 tissues in every mouse but a composite
id, −0.04354 against 0.08693 (p 0.04762). Nothing in v0.3 names the difference of composition.

**Decided, the minimum.** (i) A composition diagnostic: the shares of cell types (and tissues) per
replicate, compared between the groups at the replicate level. (ii) UNIDENTIFIABLE where a type is
absent from the replicates of one group. (iii) Verdicts per type, by default not combined. (iv) A
claim about a mixture only together with its difference of composition.

**Decided (2026-10-17).** Composition is one of the checks that carry the protection GATE 4 loses
with delta_min (change 3): it can only remove a conclusion. It is tried first on §4 itself, by the
second diagnostics (`real_pilot_diagnostics_2_plan.md`, check (v)).

**The second diagnostics, check (v)** (`real_pilot_4.log`, `real_pilot_4_summary.log`). In males
the groups' composition of cell types differs (total variation distance 0.3411, exact p 0.03175 over
126 relabelings; of tissues 0.2027, p 0.9206), but within the 35 types with at least 20 cells in at
least 3 units of each group the drop of `mi_3bin` stays: combined, with equal weights, −0.01356
(exact p 0.01587), 68.7% of the overall −0.01975; 31 of the 35 types lower. A unit's MI is not
associated with its share of fibroblast-like cells (Pearson r −0.321, p 0.3875; Spearman ρ +0.1,
p 0.81). The plan's outcome: composition does not account for the drop; the prediction («внутри
типов падение MI у самцов исчезает») does not hold. The four fibroblast-like types, where Col1a1
is expressed, do not show the drop (mean +0.008123, 2 of 4 lower); the other 31 types do (mean
−0.01636, 29 of 31 lower). In the females only 2 types qualify (the old group is the mammary gland
alone). As a check that removes a conclusion when the within-type difference vanishes, composition
would not have removed §4 in males; read per type, (iii), §4 within the fibroblast-like types shows
no drop. Which reading the engine takes is to be decided.

**Decided (2026-10-18).** Where the data carry cell-type labels, the verdict follows the combined
estimate within the claim's domain, the types weighted equally; the estimate over the whole mixture
goes into the report with the difference of composition. A claim about the coupling of a pair is
judged in the types where both genes are expressed; the threshold and its definition (in both
groups, in either, or over the pooled cells) are fixed from principle before the third diagnostics,
not from §4 (for example 10% detection, the lower bound of v1's levels), and checked there on two
cases: on §4 in males only the fibroblast-like domain should remain; in Marsh the induced coupling
must not drop out for a low detection in the group without the induction.

**The code (v0.4.0.dev0; the rule fixed before the third diagnostics, its plan).** A type is in the
domain of a claim about a pair where, in at least one of the two groups, both genes are detected in
at least 10% of the type's cells (`domain.DOMAIN_DETECTION`), detection in a group being the mean over
its replicates, each weighted equally, of the share of the replicate's cells of the type with a count
of the gene; for a set of genes, where a count of any of its genes is detected so; for a claim without
genes, every type; in each case only where the type has at least 20 cells in at least 3 replicates of
each group (in a paired design, replicates with that many cells in both groups), within each stratum.
*In at least one group*, because a claim of change may be the appearance of the coupling itself (the
Marsh case), and pooling the cells would make the rule depend on the groups' numbers of cells. The
effect is estimated on (replicate, type) units, each at equal depth within its (stratum, type): the
groups' difference per type, the types weighted equally within a stratum, the strata by precision,
with a cluster-robust standard error over the replicates (Satterthwaite df) and relabelings of the
replicates within strata (`combined.py`). The whole mixture's estimate, the estimate outside the domain
and each type's go into the report with the difference of composition. An empty domain gives
INCONCLUSIVE, cause `empty_domain`. GATE 0 and GATE 4 run on the domain's cells.

**The third diagnostics** (2026-10-18; `real_pilot_5_summary.log`, (a1) and (a2)). On §4 in males the
rule keeps 11 types: the four fibroblast-like, and seven others that enter by the young group alone,
Col1a1 detected in 10.5–12.3% of their young cells and 0–4.3% of their old ones (the
granulocytopoietic cells 10.9% and 10.4%); the owner's prediction, only the fibroblast-like domain,
does not hold. In the same run (b1) the young units' non-fibroblast types detect six of the nine
fibroblast genes more often (exact p < 0.05), and (b4) the young male units have more genes and fewer
counts per cell. On Marsh the induced coupling stays in its domain by "in at least one group" (Fos in
23.0% of ENZ-NONE's cells, 0.2% of ENZ-INHIB's; under "in both groups" it would drop out); the null
contrast's domain is empty (Fos 1.2% and 0.2%), so it is INCONCLUSIVE by `empty_domain`.

**v2 test.** Backgrounds with several cell types: nulls within types where only the composition
differs, with a metric that differs between types (a within-type claim must not be SUPPORTED);
real within-type effects that the composition masks or inflates.

## 13. GATE 5: several negative controls, judged in the metric's units

**Evidence** (the pilot, 2026-10-16; `real_pilot.log`, case 1, claim 1b: `norm_pearson` of Smad3
and Col1a1, 4 young against 4 old males). One negative control pair, Tst–Lrrc42, failed the metric
at α = 0.05: its value, −0.118, lay outside the null of unrelated pairs (p = 0.0498, null centre

and fails where it stands out (`gates.py`, `gate5_controls`). A pair that is unrelated in the sense
of the null is exchangeable with the null's pairs, so its p is uniform whatever the metric: one
pair at α = 0.05 fails a valid metric in about 5% of claims by chance alone, and a metric that
reports association between every two unrelated genes moves the null with the control, so that the
test does not see it. What says something about the metric is the level of association it reports
between unrelated genes of the claim's expression: the null centre, which v0.3 only reports.

**Decided (2026-10-16).** Several negative controls, with a rule that accounts for their number or
that works by equivalence.

**Proposed (to be decided).**
- **The controls.** k = 10 negative pairs per claim, drawn by a rule in the claim's own data before
  its metric is read on them: genes of the claim's genes' expression neighbourhoods (as the null's
  pairs are drawn now), without the claim's genes and without the pairs of a curated list of known
  interactions; the draw keyed by the claim's canonical form (change 1). A pair the user names is
  one of the k.
- **The rule, by equivalence (recommended).** GATE 5 asks whether the metric is shown to report an
  association between unrelated genes of a size that matters: the mean of the k controls and the
  null centre, with their 1 − α interval over the pairs (and the strata), against ±delta_min in the
  metric's units (change 3). FAIL where the interval lies wholly outside ±delta_min, PASS where it
  lies inside, WARN otherwise, with the interval reported. A pair that stands out on its own is
  reported, not a FAIL. Its error on a valid metric does not grow with k; more controls narrow the
  interval.
- **The alternative, by multiplicity.** Each control tested against the null of unrelated pairs as
  now, Holm over the k pairs and the K strata at α; FAIL where one survives. It keeps v0.3's
  question with a family-wise error of α where the null is right, but by the exchangeability above

**Decided (2026-10-17).** The rule is chosen after the diagnostics. The owner prefers a check of
specificity, the diagnostics' check (ii) (the claim's difference among controls of similar detection
without known interactions), to the equivalence or Holm over 10 pairs above.

**The diagnostics (2026-10-17)** (change 3): the check of specificity separated §4 from the sex
differences, with two of its four outcomes within the draw's reach of the line at 100 controls.
Among the 8 pairs that fall in males as far as Smad3–Col1a1 are Htra1–Ogn, Lum–Aebp1 and
Gas1–Sfrp1 (`real_pilot_3_summary.log`).

**Proposed (to be decided).** GATE 5's negative control becomes change 3's check of specificity.
- **It judges the claim, not the metric:** its outcome is a condition of SUPPORTED, not a field of
  metric validity. The positive control stays as now, evidence of a response where GATE 4 does not
