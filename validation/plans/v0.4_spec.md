# v0.4: the engine changes, each with the v2 test that checks it (draft for the owner)

A specification, not code: no line of `src/` changes before the owner approves it (decision of
2026-10-11). `src/` stays byte-identical to the tag `v0.3.0-prereg` until then. Every change below
names its evidence in v1 or the reviews, the proposed change, the choices left to the owner, and
the test of validation v2 that checks it (the requirements of v2: `PROJECT.md`, "Requirements for
v2", and `validation/plans/v2_requirements.md`). The numbers come from the logs named, each from a
script in this repository.

Abbreviations: Review 1 and Review 2 as in `PROJECT.md` ("Requirements for v2"); D1 is
`validation/prereg/DEVIATIONS.md` D1.

## 1. GATE 4 measures the response where the signal is claimed

**Evidence.** R1's Xist claim was called "metric invalid" by GATE 4, falsely (D1). The module probe
raised Xist 2-fold in every cell and measured the metric over all cells. For a mean over cells
that response is, by identity, the share of cells with the marker times the response in them:
+0.163 over all cells against delta_min 0.25, +0.631 over the 25.8% of B2's cells with Xist; with
`anchors.py`'s constants the probe needed at least 0.25 / 0.631 = 39.6% of cells with Xist
(`validation/exploratory/gate4_module_analytic.log`; Review 1 B8 and B10). GATE 4's interval is a
Monte Carlo interval over 200 injections into one dataset, not an uncertainty over donors
(Review 1 B9).

**Change.** GATE 4 injects the signal the way the claim states it, into one group, and measures
the response with the claim's own statistic: the replicate-level difference between the groups
that the effect test uses (`effect._rep_values`, `effect._infer`), injected against its reference.
For Xist the signal goes into the female group's cells, where Xist is, so the response is close to
the response in the cells that carry it. The interval of the response is taken over the replicates
(the injection repeated within each replicate), so that it is an uncertainty over donors.

**For the owner.** (a) Inject into the claimed group only, or into both groups in opposite
directions (a larger contrast at the same dose). (b) Whether to keep, next to it, the present
all-cell response as a reported diagnostic.

**v2 test.** Valid mean-expression metrics of markers present in only some cells (10–40%) and of
markers present in all cells of one group (sex markers), with the truth about validity from a
mechanism other than GATE 4's (requirement 6): a false "metric invalid" on them at most at its
nominal; the module probe on valid metrics, not only on a useless one (in v1 it ran only on N6c).

## 2. A module probe that creates no response by losing counts

**Evidence.** The module probe's sham thins every gene, the module's too, while the injection keeps
the module's counts. At a depth of 2,000 counts a marker of single counts gives a response of
0.360, of which the sham's loss of the single counts makes 0.178 and the 2-fold change 0.182
(`gate4_module_analytic.log`): on shallow data the probe passes for the wrong reason. With fewer
counts the response exceeds the identity's bound and even ln 2 (0.652 against 0.619 in the
marker's cells at a CP10k of 6; 1.20 for single counts at a depth of 2,000).

**Change (proposed).** A sham with the same loss of depth and no change of composition has to thin
the module genes too, so with thinning alone it always adds count noise to them. The proposal is
to inject the change of composition by thinning the module genes in the comparison group (a
`fold`-times decrease there, the counts staying real), against the untouched data and without a
sham: the depth then changes only by the module's share of the counts, which is reported, and the
loss of counts is the signal itself, as it is for a real decrease of expression. The coupling
probe keeps its sham: its injection and sham thin each gene with the same marginal keep
probability, so their loss of counts is the same.

**For the owner.** (a) This design, or the present one with the sham's module noise subtracted by
a second sham. (b) Whether a decrease in one group, or an increase made by thinning everything
else, is the default direction.

**v2 test.** Markers of single counts at depths from 1,000 to 20,000 counts per cell, valid and
blind by the independent truth, through the module probe: GATE 4's response must not exceed the
response of the composition change alone (computed by the oracle on the counts before thinning),
and a blind metric must not pass at a low depth.

## 3. The probe's strength tied to the SESOI

**Evidence.** In v1 GATE 4's dose and delta_min (0.5 × SESOI) came from the pilot, that is, from the
oracle (the pilot's saturation dose; README, limitations). A user has no pilot. The module probe's
default (2-fold in 30% of cells) cannot pass a well-detected mean-expression metric at a SESOI of
0.5: at most 0.3 × ln 2 = 0.208 against delta_min 0.25, by arithmetic (`gate4_module_dilution.log`).

**Change.** The probe's size is defined on the construct's scale and equals the claim's SESOI, so
that GATE 4 asks whether the metric shows at least half (delta_min) of a change that matters:
- module: fold = exp(SESOI) for a SESOI on the log scale, injected into the claimed group's cells
  (change 1);
- coupling: the strength at which the injected shared factor raises the correlation of the two
  genes' log expected expression by the SESOI, given their biological variances estimated from
  the data with the counting noise removed (a calculation on the data, not a pilot).

**For the owner.** The coupling rule needs a model of the genes' biological variance; the
alternative is a dose scan reported as a curve, with validity judged at the dose whose construct
change equals the SESOI.

**v2 test.** The engine run with these defaults, no oracle settings: the rates of false "metric
invalid" on valid metrics and of PASS on blind ones, per level, against the independent truth.

## 4. A rule for the SESOI and the dose without the oracle

**Evidence.** v1's SESOI per background and level was the pilot's: the smallest value of a grid
(0.005–1.0) at which the oracle reaches a correct definite outcome (NO DETECTABLE EFFECT by the TOST,
or NOT SUPPORTED against the direction) on at least 95% of the pilot's datasets of the background's
null (N1 on B1, N7 on B2; v1.md section 3.2). It needs only the spread of the metric over
replicates, which the user's own data give.

**Change.** The user declares the SESOI. The engine reports the design's floor: the smallest SESOI
at which this design confirms absence by the TOST with a power fixed in advance (v1's rule used
0.95), from the replicate-level spread of the metric within the groups and the replicates per
group. A declared SESOI below the floor is
flagged (absence cannot be confirmed); without a declared SESOI the floor is used and the report
says so. The dose follows from the SESOI (change 3).

**For the owner.** Whether a missing SESOI should default to the floor or keep v0.3's behaviour (no
TOST, SUPPORTED withheld where a bias cannot be sized).

**v2 test.** Real effects and nulls run with the floor as the SESOI and with a SESOI declared below
it; the rates of NO DETECTABLE EFFECT on effects of the size of the floor and the flags, per level.

## 5. Capture that varies from cell to cell (N8)

**Evidence.** The prediction that v0.3 fails S1 on N8 was not confirmed: 11 false SUPPORTED of 790
(limit 29). At the high level 11/253 = 4.3% (95% CI 2.2–7.6%) against a nominal 2.5%, Holm 0.61
over the twelve high-level null cells: not an established excess
(`v1_null_cells.log`). The errors cluster by gene pair: 6 of the 11 on one pair (B1:7, 6 of its 32
N8 datasets), the design effect by pair 3.91, and at it S1's N8 cell passes a doubled error with
probability 0.211 (`v1_pair_deff.log`). v0.3's depth correction thins a whole group by one ratio,
so it cannot remove a per-cell spread of capture. The decisive experiment's rates on N8 are in
`v1_analyzers.log`.

**Change (proposed).** For a composition estimand, an optional per-cell equalization: every cell
thinned to a common target depth (a pre-registered quantile of the pooled per-cell depths, cells
below it left out), with each cell's keep probability computed from its depth over the genes the
metric does not read. The development set showed why: a keep probability computed from a total that
includes the metric's genes distorts their joint distribution (quantile-matched thinning left 18%
of a pure depth artifact, `equalize.thin_to_match`'s docstring). GATE 1 reports the spread of
per-cell depth within each group's replicates next to the means.

**For the owner.** (a) Per-cell equalization by default for a composition estimand, or only when
GATE 1 finds the spreads unequal. (b) The target quantile, and whether cells below it are dropped
or kept unthinned.

**v2 test.** N8 and variants (Beta spreads of capture, one group and both) sized on the cards at
risk to detect 4.3% against 2.5% with power 0.9 after the design effect by pair; the real effects
E1–E3 to measure what the per-cell thinning costs in sensitivity; the development set's MI depth
artifact to show the joint distribution is kept.

## 6. Tissue and cell-type composition

**Evidence.** v1 used one cell type per background (oligodendrocytes in B1, beta cells in B2), so it
did not test a claim whose groups differ in the shares of cell types or tissues. The target claims
compare tissues or mixtures (ageing changes cell-type shares). v0.3 stratifies only by the factors
a user declares (`within`) and does not check composition.

**Change.** (i) A composition diagnostic: the shares of cell types (and tissues) per replicate,
compared between the groups at the replicate level, reported next to GATE 1. (ii) The estimand
says which effect is claimed: within type (the default when labels are given: the effect estimated
within each type, with a permutation over replicates stratified by type, and combined) or of the
mixture as sampled (the composition difference reported with it). (iii) UNIDENTIFIABLE when a type
does not occur in both groups' replicates.

**For the owner.** How the within-type effects are combined (equal weights, or by the replicates'
cells), and whether a mixture claim is allowed at all.

**v2 test.** Backgrounds with several cell types: nulls within types where only the composition
differs, with a metric that differs between types (a within-type claim must not be SUPPORTED); real
within-type effects that the composition masks or inflates.

## Proposed beyond the owner's list (for decision)

- **Sparse input.** `SimpleData` accepts only dense arrays (`core.py:122`), which is why R1 never
  reached the engine in v1 (D1); a user's AnnData is usually sparse. v2 test: every anchor run end
  to end on its real, sparse data before the tag (requirement 14).
- **The module probe in the CLI and the MCP server**, which offer only the coupling probe.
- **GATE 0's refusals on null data.** On N7 GATE 0 refused 26 of 276 high-level cards (9.4%), 29 of
  the 31 refusals on three gene pairs, on data without a difference in dropout between the groups,
  and S3's N7 cell had 204 of 237 correct definite outcomes against 250/271 on N1
  (`b2_depth_by_chemistry.log`). No change is proposed; whether a bias that does not differ between
  the groups should block is a question for the owner.
