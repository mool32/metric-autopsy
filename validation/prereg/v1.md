# Pre-registration v1 — confirmatory validation of metric-autopsy v0.3

**Status: FINAL DRAFT for approval by the project owner (2026-10-08).** It incorporates the
owner's decisions of 2026-10-08 (section 10). Nothing is frozen until the owner approves this
file. On approval: the engine, this file and the panel code of section 2 are frozen under the tag
`v0.3.0-prereg`, and the sha256 of every frozen file is recorded in the tag message and in
`PROJECT.md`. After the tag, the engine and the protocol change only through
`validation/prereg/DEVIATIONS.md` (D-series, one entry per change: what, why, when, and which
results it touches). Every number cited from the validation comes from the scripts named here,
with the hashes from their JSON reports.

Scope: RNA only (scRNA-seq counts). Other modalities are out of v1.

---

## 1. What the validation tests

The claim under test is about the validator, not about any biology:

> On datasets whose truth is known by construction or by design, metric-autopsy v0.3 (frozen)
> (a) rarely certifies a claim that is false — false SUPPORTED on nulls, artifacts and useless
> metrics; (b) rarely gives a verdict that is wrong *given the design* — outside the set of
> verdicts that are correct for that design; and (c) commits to a definite verdict where the
> design makes the truth establishable, instead of retreating to INCONCLUSIVE.

(c) exists because a validator that never errs because it never commits is useless. Both axes are
primary.

The development set (`validation/probes/`) found the bugs and the fixes were developed against
it. It plays no part here. The panel below has no overlap with it: different generators,
different backgrounds (real data), and a blind assignment of cases.

## 3. The panel

### 3.1 Backgrounds: selection criteria now, selection after the network opens

Each background is chosen **mechanically** by the criteria below: the agent lists the candidates
that meet every criterion, with the facts that decide each, applies the choice rule, downloads
the file, and records source, version and sha256 in `backgrounds.json`, committed before any
panel dataset exists. A background for which no candidate qualifies is dropped with the cases
that need it; the drop is recorded (after the tag, as a D-series entry). Nothing is substituted by
judgment.

| ID | Use | Criteria (all required) | Choice rule | Candidate |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| B1 | nulls and injected signals N1–N6, E1–E3 | droplet 3' platform (10x Chromium); one annotated cell type with ≥ 200 cells in each of ≥ 24 donors; raw UMI counts; donor IDs in the metadata; public download without registration | most donors meeting the cell threshold; ties: more cells per donor | human skin fibroblasts, CELLxGENE (`DATASETS.md`, 179 donors) |
| B2 | mouse-level null N7, sex anchor R1 | droplet 3' platform; one tissue and cell type with ≥ 12 mice of ≥ 200 cells each, both sexes; raw counts; mouse IDs; public | the tissue with the most such mice; ties: more cells per mouse | Tabula Muris Senis, droplet |
| B3 | cell-cycle and content anchors R2 | plate-based capture with ERCC spike-ins; phase assigned by an independent measurement (DNA content sort), not from the transcriptome; ≥ 50 cells per phase; raw counts including ERCC; public | most cells per phase | Buettner et al. 2015, mESC G1 / S / G2M (E-MTAB-2805) |
| B4 | FUCCI anchor R3 | FUCCI reporter intensities for every cell (phase labels independent of the transcriptome); full-length scRNA-seq with raw counts; ≥ 300 cells; plate IDs; public | most cells with FUCCI intensities; ties: more plates | Mahdessian et al. 2021, U2OS FUCCI, Smart-seq2 |

Fixed before the key (`panel.plan_background`): every background keeps its 2,000 most expressed
genes plus the named genes; the analysed pair is the most correlated pair of log-normalized
expression among the 300 most expressed genes other than mitochondrial and ribosomal ones; the
positive control is the next most correlated disjoint pair; the negative control is the pair
closest to the analysed pair in mean expression with |r| < 0.02; N6c's 50 random genes are drawn
from the kept genes with a fixed public seed. Within a dataset donors (mice) are drawn without
replacement, 200 cells each; across datasets of a condition they are reused (B1 has 179 donors);
donor overlap is reported, and every rate is recomputed on donor-disjoint subsets as a
sensitivity analysis.

### 3.2 Cases

The **allowed set** is the set of verdicts that are correct *given the design*; anything outside
it is an error. A verdict is scored by its label, the text before " — " (SUPPORTED covers
"SUPPORTED (provisional until replicated)" and "SUPPORTED — replicated"); bracketed qualifiers
(parametric only, underpowered, non-directional) do not change the label.

A case is **establishable** when the *oracle* (`oracle.py`) reaches its definite verdict with
power ≥ 0.9 in the pilot, on datasets drawn with public seeds outside the panel's; the engine's
**definite** verdict is then expected. The oracle is told what the engine has to find out: it
takes the metric's validity as known, removes a planted capture loss by thinning the other side
with the true factor and a planted dropout by applying it to the other side, and tests the
per-donor metric values with the test the graded replicate rule prescribes for the design (exact
permutation over donors at ≥ 4 per group, Welch at 3; equivalence by TOST, the (1 − 2α) interval
inside ±SESOI), at α = 0.05. For a useless metric (N6) the oracle is the valid metric facing the
same injected signal (log-normalized Pearson for the coupling, a G2M score for the module), which
must respond by more than 4 standard errors. The oracle's power is the most a validator could
reach; the engine's own verdicts never decide establishability, which would make decisiveness true
by selection. Group labels are neutral (`A` / `B`); the side that receives an artifact or a signal
is drawn by the key.

The metric of every norm_pearson case is the engine's log-normalized Pearson of the analysed pair,
with the positive and negative control pairs (GATE 5) and an injected coupling of the pair (GATE 4).
Since the decisions of 2026-10-08 a depth bias of this metric does not block where the declared
composition correction removes depth between groups (probe p13).

### 3.3 Blind assignment

`panel.py --key-seed <seed>` builds every dataset of sections N and E: the seed decides each
dataset's condition, variant, side and seed, and the order of the dataset IDs (`panel.assign`).
The **key seed is generated and held by the project owner** and never enters the repository or
this session before scoring. For each ID the script writes the counts (`<ID>.npz`: counts, the
neutral groups, donors, totals) and a claim card (`<ID>.json`: metric, gene pair, control pairs,
injected signal, groups `A`/`B`, replicate column, and the pre-registration — composition
estimand, direction, SESOI, `judgment_pending: false`), and a manifest with the sha256 of both.
Claim cards of the norm_pearson cases are identical across conditions except for the ID and the
direction (`test_claim_cards_do_not_reveal_the_condition`). Claims are **directional**: an
effect's card declares its true direction (the signal side is the higher one); a null's card a
direction drawn by the key, so the card does not reveal the condition. The truth is generated by
`panel.py`'s own binomial thinning (as in seqgendiff, Gerard 2020).

The frozen engine runs once per claim card (`run_panel.py`), through the Python API with the card
as the pre-registration. Every run is in the run log, and the reports and the log are committed —
with their sha256 in the commit message — **before** the key is revealed. Then the owner reveals
the seed and `score.py` decodes the conditions and computes the outcomes. Anchors R1–R3 are not
blinded (their truth is public); the engine is frozen before they are run.

## 4. Replicates, precision and compute

**Replicates.** The success criteria follow one principle (section 6). For a false SUPPORTED rate
whose nominal value is α/2 = 0.025 it needs at least 506 datasets per condition (`oc.log`); 400
would pass a sound validator with probability 0.79 only. Each key null condition (N1, N2 at
c = 0.5, N5, N6c) therefore gets **600** datasets (P(pass) 0.92 for a sound validator, 0.032 for
one with double the error). Other conditions keep 50–200 datasets: they enter the pooled criteria,
the dose–verdict curves and P4.

**Timing pilot** (`timing.py` → `timing.log`, engine at the commit recorded there, simulated data
of B1's planned size: 3,200 cells × 2,000 genes, a sample of 24 cards with the panel's mix of
conditions, on a 4-core cloud container with one other job running): 32.3 s per claim card on one
core (N5 14 s, the other conditions 29–37 s), no engine errors; three worker processes ran the
same cards 3.1 times faster (10.5 s of wall time per card), so the speed-up is close to linear.

**Compute plan.** `run_panel.py` runs one claim card per worker process (BLAS threads pinned to 1).
At the measured rate the 4,450 cards take about 40 CPU-hours: 13 hours of wall time with 3
workers, about 5 with 8, about 2.5 with 16. Budget: the panel must finish within 72
hours of wall time on the machine it is run on, with at least 8 worker processes. The pilot
(section 8, step 3) re-times 24 cards on the real B1 before the key is generated. If the
projection exceeds the budget, conditions are dropped — never replicates of the remaining ones —
in this order until it fits: the N3 steps; the E1 doses other than the key dose; the N2 steps other
than c = 0.5; N4. The key null conditions and E1–E3 at the key dose are never dropped. A drop is
recorded before the key is generated (after the tag, as a D-series entry).

## 5. Primary outcomes

Every rate is reported as k/n with its two-sided 95% Clopper-Pearson interval, per condition and
pooled (`score.py`).

- **P1 False SUPPORTED** on nulls, artifacts and useless metrics (N1–N7): share of verdicts
  labelled SUPPORTED. With directional claims a two-sided test at α gives α/2 on a null with a
  valid metric.
- **P2 Outside the allowed set**, all claim cards (N and E). A missing report or an engine error
  counts as outside the set.
- **P3 Decisiveness** on establishable cards: share of definite verdicts (anything but
  INCONCLUSIVE). Also reported: share of *correct* definite verdicts.
- **P4 Per-gate sensitivity and specificity**:
  - GATE 1: flags the group with a capture loss (N2/N3 steps), and stays silent on N1;
  - GATE 2 / effect field: "explained by depth" on N2 at c ≤ 0.5; share of the effect retained on
    E1–E3;
  - GATE 4: PASS for the valid metric on N1/E1, FAIL for N6a and N6c;
  - GATE 5: FAIL rate of the negative control on N1 (specificity);
  - GATE 0: blocking biases on the valid metric (specificity); attenuation reported, never failed;
    depth biases removed by the correction, reported.
- **Dose–verdict curves** (E1 doses, N2 and N3 steps): share of each verdict with its interval.

## 6. Success criteria

**Principle** (decided 2026-10-08): every criterion passes a sound validator with probability
≥ 0.90 and a validator whose error is double the nominal with probability ≤ 0.05. Nominal error:
α/2 = 0.025 for a false SUPPORTED (directional claims) and for a verdict outside the allowed set
(dominated by the same false SUPPORTED); for decisiveness, 0.15 (decisiveness 0.85: establishable
cases have oracle power ≥ 0.90, and a validator must also demonstrate the metric's response, which
the oracle is told), doubled 0.30. For each criterion the threshold is the most lenient one at
which the doubled-error validator still passes with probability ≤ 0.05 (`oc.py`); a rule "at most
k* errors of n" is the same as "upper 95% Clopper-Pearson bound ≤ T" for the T in `oc.log`.

| Criterion | n | Passes if | P(pass), sound | P(pass), doubled error |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **S1** false SUPPORTED, each key null condition (N1, N2 c = 0.5, N5, N6c) | 600 each | at most 20 of 600 (upper bound ≤ 5.1%) | 0.92 at 2.5% | 0.032 at 5% |
| **S2** false SUPPORTED, all null cards | 3,550 | at most 155 (upper bound ≤ 5.09%) | 1.00 at 2.5% | 0.043 at 5% |
| **S3** decisiveness, establishable cards | n_est (from the pilot, ≥ 200) | at least k*(n_est) definite verdicts; k* = 152 at n = 200 (lower bound ≥ 69.5%) | 1.00 at 85% | ≤ 0.05 at 70% |
| **S4** outside the allowed set, all cards | 4,450 | at most 198 (upper bound ≤ 5.1%) | 1.00 at 2.5% | 0.047 at 5% |

If conditions are dropped (section 4), the n and k* of S2–S4 are recomputed by `oc.py` for the
remaining cards before the key is generated. The validation **passes** only if S1–S4 all hold.
Each failed criterion is reported with the number that failed it; there is no rescue language and
no re-run.

## 7. Secondary outcomes

Anchors R1–R3 (verdict against the allowed set, one by one, with the four fields); P4; the
dose–verdict curves; the donor-disjoint sensitivity analysis; run time and memory per card.

## 10. Decisions

**Decided by the project owner on 2026-10-08** (incorporated above):
1. S1–S4, the replicate rule "fewer conditions, not fewer replicates", establishability through
   the oracle; a timing pilot with parallel runs and a compute plan in this file; the oracle and
   `panel.py` with their own tests, committed before the tag.
2. Directional claims: the direction is mandatory in the pre-registration; SUPPORTED only for an
   effect in the declared direction; a sign reversed by the correction stays INCONCLUSIVE;
   non-directional claims allowed and marked. Every threshold recomputed by the principle of
   section 6; the number of datasets that needs (600 per key null condition) is stated in section 4.
3. Positive-control dose 2.0 and power threshold 0.8.
4. Selection criteria for B1–B4 now (section 3.1), the selection mechanical after the network opens.
5. GATE 0's null uses the depth-matched draw of GATE 5, with a regression test.
6. GATE 0, option (b): a depth bias does not block where the declared correction equalizes depth
   between groups; dropout and library-scale biases block only if the bias exceeds a tolerance in
   SESOI units and so does the lower bound of its 95% interval; otherwise reported; p13 is a
   dev-set regression.
7. The limitation of "explained by depth" is written in `paper/drafts/limitations.md`.

**For approval in this final draft:**
- a. Without a SESOI a nuisance bias cannot be sized: the engine reports it as unsized and
  withholds SUPPORTED (INCONCLUSIVE). This is the agent's reading of "otherwise, a message
  without blocking" — every claim card of the panel declares a SESOI, so it does not touch the
  panel, but it governs users who declare none.
- b. The bias tolerance default: 0.5 SESOI (`bias_tolerance`).
- c. The nominal decisiveness 0.85 behind S3, and the threshold rule of section 6 (the most
  lenient threshold at which a doubled-error validator still passes with probability ≤ 0.05).
- d. 600 datasets per key null condition; the compute budget (72 hours of wall time, ≥ 8 workers)
  and the drop order of section 4.
