## 1. What the validation tests

The claim under test is about the validator, not about any biology:

> On datasets whose truth is known by construction or by design, metric-autopsy v0.3 (frozen)
> (a) rarely certifies a claim that is false — false SUPPORTED on nulls, artifacts, useless or
> blind metrics; (b) rarely gives a verdict that is wrong *for its reason* — a label and cause
> outside the pairs that are correct for that design and that metric; (c) rarely calls a valid
> metric invalid; and (d) commits to a correct definite verdict where the design makes the truth
> establishable, instead of retreating to INCONCLUSIVE.

(d) exists because a validator that never errs because it never commits is useless. All four are
primary.

**Scope of inference.** The rates generalize to repeated samples (donors and cells) from the two
backgrounds B1 and B2; to the artifacts of the binomial-thinning family (capture loss, dropout)
plus N8's per-cell variable capture, which lies outside the engine's correction family; and to one
metric, log-normalized Pearson of a gene pair, at three expression levels where it is valid,
blind or in between (with three useless metrics, N6). Other metrics — `mi_3bin` included —,
other backgrounds and other artifacts are outside v1.

The development set (`validation/probes/`) found the bugs and the fixes were developed against
it. It plays no part here. The panel uses the same mechanisms as the development set (binomial
thinning, dropout, an injected coupling); what differs is the code (written separately; it never
imports the engine), the backgrounds (real data, chosen by a rule after the tag) and the
assignment of cases (by a public random value that does not exist when the code is frozen).

## 3. The panel

### 3.1 Backgrounds and the pair pool

Each background is chosen **mechanically** by the criteria below, by `select_backgrounds.py` in the
workflow after the tag: every candidate that meets every criterion is listed with the facts that
decide it (`selection.md`), the choice rule picks one, the chosen cells are extracted and written
with their sha256 to `backgrounds.json`, and both files are committed before any panel dataset
exists. B1 and B2 come from the CELLxGENE Census, its `stable` release at the time (recorded); B3
and B4 are named candidates from their public sources. A background for which no candidate
qualifies is dropped with the cases that need it; the drop is recorded: without B2, N7 and R1 — the
pilot writes `B2:*` to pilot.json's drops and every later step leaves N7 out (the second review
found the pilot stopping instead; `test_a_background_without_a_candidate_is_dropped_with_its_cases`);
without B1 there is no validation to run, and the pilot stops. Nothing is substituted by judgment. The selection's code ran before the tag only as a rehearsal that prints no identities.

| ID | Use | Criteria (all required) | Choice rule | Extracted |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| B1 | nulls and injected signals N1–N6, N8, E1–E3 | human; droplet 3' (the Census assays 10x 3' v1, v2 and v3 and "10x 3' transcription profiling"); primary cells; one cell type of one dataset with ≥ 200 cells in each of ≥ 24 donors; donor IDs; raw counts; public | most such donors; ties: more cells per donor, then the IDs in order | at most 200 of the qualifying donors and 400 cells per donor (samples with the public seed 20261008) |
| B2 | mouse-level null N7, sex anchor R1 | mouse; droplet 3' (as B1); primary cells; one tissue and cell type of one dataset with ≥ 12 mice of ≥ 200 cells each, both sexes among them; raw counts; mouse IDs; public | most such mice; ties: more cells per mouse, then the IDs | all qualifying mice, at most 400 cells each |
| B3 | cell-cycle and content anchors R2 | plate-based capture with ERCC spike-ins; phase by an independent measurement (DNA-content sort); ≥ 50 cells per phase; raw counts including ERCC; public | the named candidate | Buettner et al. 2015, mESC G1 / S / G2M (E-MTAB-2805) |
| B4 | FUCCI anchor R3 | FUCCI intensities for every cell; full-length scRNA-seq with raw counts; ≥ 300 cells; plate IDs; public | the named candidate | Mahdessian et al. 2021, U2OS FUCCI (GEO GSE146773: `GSE146773_Counts.csv.gz`, `GSE146773_fucci_coords.csv.gz`) |

Raw counts and the pair pool (below), criteria of B1 and B2 that only the extracted matrix shows,
are checked on the extraction — non-negative integers, and `panel.plan_background` finds a pool of k
≥ 4 pairs per level — in the order of the choice rule over at most the first five candidates: the
first that meets both is chosen, and the ranks that fail are listed with the reason. B3 and B4 are
checked by the same code in the rehearsal before the tag: B3 qualifies (96 cells per phase, 100 ERCC
rows, raw counts); B4's deposit has plate IDs, but FUCCI intensities for only 1,146 of its 1,152
cells and RSEM expected counts (2,202,784 non-integer values), so **B4 and R3 are dropped by the
raw-counts criterion and by "FUCCI intensities for every cell"** unless the deposit changes (R3 has
no runner). The extraction caps keep a background small enough for every shard to hold it (B1 at
most 80,000 cells of 2,000 kept genes); they are samples by the public seed, decided before the
choice is known. The Census is counted in chunks on its category codes, never cell by cell as
strings.

**The pair pool**, fixed before the key (`panel.plan_background`; public seed 20261008):
- Only donors with ≥ 200 cells are used. Gene symbols are made unique on loading, as anndata's
  `var_names_make_unique` does (a second occurrence of a symbol becomes `symbol-1`): several Ensembl
  genes can share a symbol, and the engine refuses duplicate names, which would have made every card
  of such a background an engine error (the second review). A gene is eligible for a pair if it is
  not mitochondrial or ribosomal by its symbol (`MT-`, `RPS`, `RPL`, which also leaves out a few
  other genes such as `RPS6KA1`) and is detected in ≥ 5% of the cells of every donor.
- **Levels** by mean count per cell and detection: *low* — detected in 10% to 50% of cells;
  *medium* — in at least 50%, mean < 2; *high* — in at least 50%, mean ≥ 2. In each level the 1,000
  most expressed eligible genes enter the search.
- A pair's correlation is the pooled within-donor correlation of log-normalized expression over
  all cells with the log library size partialled out, on 200 cells per donor. Per level the
  **k most correlated disjoint pairs** form the pool: **k = 8** where every level of the
  background has 8 pairs with their negative controls (a level needs at least 4k eligible genes:
  a pair and its control take four; the high level 4(k + 1), as it gives the positive control
  too), otherwise the largest common k ≥ 4 (below 4 the candidate does not qualify, and the next
  one in the choice order is taken).
- **Controls.** Each pool pair has its own negative control: of the 10 unused pairs of its level
  closest to it in mean expression whose partial correlation is below 0.02 in absolute value, the
  one whose log-normalized Pearson is most typical of GATE 5's matched null on the plan's cells
  (the smallest |z| against 200 pairs drawn from the two genes' expression neighbourhoods,
  GATE 5's own rule, among 2,000 genes as a dataset keeps them: the pair, the candidate and the
  most expressed, the other named genes not being chosen yet; ties: the closest). The third review
  found controls chosen by
  partial correlation alone standing out of that null on some pairs (|z| 2 to 3.4), so that GATE 5
  failed on those pairs' null datasets far more often than the sound validator's model allows. The
  positive control (GATE 5) is one pair per background, the next most correlated disjoint pair of
  the high level.
- The kept genes are the named genes and then the most expressed, 2,000 in all; N6c's 50 random
  genes are drawn from the kept genes other than the named ones (the pairs, their controls and the
  G2M genes); the G2M genes present are kept.
- Within a dataset donors (mice) are drawn without replacement, 200 cells each; across datasets
  they are reused; section 5 fixes how shared donors enter the intervals.

The plan is computed from exact integer sums, with correlations rounded to 10 decimals before
ranking, so a dense and a sparse copy of the same counts give the same pool (tested).

### 3.2 Cases, the truth and the allowed outcomes

**Scored by cause, not by label** (decided 2026-10-08). Every report carries the verdict and its
cause (`report.decide_cause`), and each card is scored as one **outcome**, a (label, cause) pair:

| Outcome | Label | Engine causes |
|---|---|---|
| SUPPORTED | SUPPORTED | `replicated`, `provisional` |
| NO DETECTABLE EFFECT | NO DETECTABLE EFFECT | `no_detectable_effect` |
| INCONCLUSIVE | INCONCLUSIVE | any INCONCLUSIVE cause |
| NOT SUPPORTED: metric invalid | NOT SUPPORTED | `metric_invalid_gate4`, `metric_invalid_gate5` |
| NOT SUPPORTED: explained by depth | NOT SUPPORTED | `explained_by_depth` |
| NOT SUPPORTED: opposite direction | NOT SUPPORTED | `opposite_direction` |
| REFUSAL (GATE 0) | NOT SUPPORTED | `metric_invalid_gate0` (a blocking nuisance bias) |
| DEGENERATE METRIC | DEGENERATE METRIC | `degenerate_metric` |

A label that does not match its cause, or a cause the engine does not have, is an error (a
rule violation, S7a; `panel.outcome`); a missing report or an exception of the engine is a
**crash**, which gives no verdict (S7b; the owner's fifth round).

**The truth about the metric on every pair** (the oracle, `oracle.pair_truth`). For each pool pair
of each background, its population response to GATE 4's injection: the coupling of the pair injected
into whole null datasets of the background's design (N1 on B1, N7 on B2) at its level's saturation
dose, minus the matched sham, averaged over 60 datasets × 20 injections. Where a condition changes
the pair's counts — capture loss (N2, E2, E3), extra dropout (N3), variable capture (N8), the
injected coupling itself (E1) — or the dataset's size — N4's 2 × 3 donors and N5's 8 donors, whose
GATE 4 is less precise than on N1's 2 × 8 (the third review) — the response and GATE 4's odds are
measured on that condition's own datasets, per variant (40 datasets × 10 injections per pair,
`oracle.case_truth`; decided after the first review, which found these responses well below N1's on
simulated backgrounds), so that a validator that shows the metric blind there is scored against the
truth of those data; the other conditions (N1, N6, N7) share their background's null, its truth and
GATE 4's odds there. The **saturation dose** of a background's level is the largest dose of the
grid 0.25–4.0 without saturation: the smallest grid dose whose level-mean response (8 datasets × 4
injections per pair and dose) reaches 95% of the grid's maximum (the largest dose where the maximum
is not above 0). With **δ\_min = 0.5 × SESOI** of the pair's level (the default of GATE 4's rule,
section 3.4), the metric is **valid** on the pair where its response is ≥ 1.2 δ\_min, **blind**
where it is ≤ 0.8 δ\_min, and **ambiguous** in between. The useless metrics of N6 are useless by
construction.

**The allowed outcomes**, the same rule for N1–N8 and E1–E3 without exceptions (`panel.allowed`):

| Truth about the metric | Data | Allowed outcomes |
|---|---|---|
| blind, or useless (N6) | any | NOT SUPPORTED: metric invalid; INCONCLUSIVE; for the constant (N6b) also DEGENERATE METRIC; for a blind metric where an artifact is planted (N2, N3, N8, E2, E3) also NOT SUPPORTED: explained by depth |
| valid | null (no biology) | NO DETECTABLE EFFECT; INCONCLUSIVE; NOT SUPPORTED: opposite direction; NOT SUPPORTED: explained by depth only where an artifact is planted (N2, N3, N8) |
| valid | real effect, \|Δ\*\| < SESOI | SUPPORTED; NO DETECTABLE EFFECT; INCONCLUSIVE; NOT SUPPORTED: explained by depth only where an artifact is planted (E2, E3) |
| valid | real effect, \|Δ\*\| ≥ SESOI | SUPPORTED; INCONCLUSIVE |
| ambiguous | any | the union of the blind row and the valid row for its data |

A REFUSAL (GATE 0's block for a nuisance bias) is allowed everywhere and is never a definite
verdict. SUPPORTED and NO DETECTABLE EFFECT are errors for a blind metric: a metric that cannot see
the effect cannot establish its absence. **Explained by depth on a blind metric** is correct where
an artifact is planted, as on a valid one (decided in the owner's fifth round: a metric blind to the
coupling still responds to depth, and that diagnosis is right; the engine checks depth before the
metric's validity), and an error where none is (S4 on the blind or useless metrics). NOT SUPPORTED: metric invalid (GATE 4 or GATE 5) is an
error on a valid metric in every condition. **Definite** outcomes are SUPPORTED, NO DETECTABLE
EFFECT, the three NOT SUPPORTED causes other than the refusal, DEGENERATE METRIC (and
UNIDENTIFIABLE, which no card should give).

**Δ\* and the real effects.** For every pool pair of B1 the pilot computes Δ\*, the population
difference of the metric under the injected coupling in the version of the condition without its
artifact — E1 at each of its doses; E2 and E3 at the base dose and at the depth their analysis runs
at, both sides thinned to half capture after the coupling and the sham (the correction of depth
brings the side that kept its depth to the other's; the fourth review found Δ\* at full depth
overstating the effect their data carry — by its measurement on simulated data they carry 0.12 to
0.69 of it —, so that correct outcomes on those data were counted as errors) — as the mean of paired draws (one donor's 200
cells with the coupling and with the sham, 2,000 draws per pair and dose), with its standard
error; the card's direction is the sign of Δ\* (the signal side is the higher one where Δ\* > 0).

**Per background and level, from the pilot** (one rule, computed on each background, nothing carried
from B1 to B2): the SESOI — the smallest value of the grid 0.005–1.0 at which the oracle reaches a
correct definite outcome on the background's null (N1 on B1, N7 on B2: NO DETECTABLE EFFECT by the
TOST, or NOT SUPPORTED against the declared direction, by the oracle's whole decision order below —
the fourth review found its depth and sign checks left out there) on at least 95% of the pilot's
datasets,
counted exactly (decided after the first review: the null itself must be establishable; where no
grid value reaches it, the grid's largest, 1.0, recorded as not found in `sesoi_found`); the
saturation dose (above). **On B1, per level:** the key dose — the smallest grid dose at which the
oracle (the metric's validity taken as known) detects E1 in the direction of Δ\* with power ≥ 0.9
**and** the level's Δ\* (the mean over its pairs) is ≥ 1.25 × SESOI; in this scan each dose's level
Δ\* comes from 2,000 paired draws in all, about 250 per pair (the third review: the Δ\* the cards use
is then computed at the chosen dose with 2,000 per pair). The real effects of a level use
its key dose as their base dose, and where no dose meets both, the level's saturation dose (its E
cases are then scored by the same rule, and their establishability comes from the oracle). The pilot
also measures, per level, the oracle's power to detect N2's raw difference at c = 0.5, and lists the
levels where it reaches 0.9 (`informative_levels`: there N2 tests the engine's correction of depth);
both are reported, and no criterion uses them.

**The oracle** (`oracle.py`) is told what the engine has to find out. For a valid or ambiguous
metric it takes the metric's validity as known, removes a planted nuisance by applying it to the
other side as well (capture loss with the true factor, dropout with the true fraction, N8's per-cell
capture with fresh Beta(2, 2) draws), and tests the per-donor metric values with the test the graded
replicate rule prescribes (permutation over donors at ≥ 4 per group — exact up to 20,000
assignments, else 1,000 random ones from a fixed seed, as on N7 with many mice: the engine's
numbers, which the fourth review found at 4,999 here —, Welch at 3; sign
flips for the paired N5; equivalence by TOST, the (1 − 2α) interval inside ±SESOI), at α = 0.05 two-sided,
in the engine's order: a detected raw difference that the true correction removes (less than half
retained, and the corrected effect shown smaller than the SESOI by the TOST, as in the engine since
journal D7) is explained by depth; one whose sign the correction reverses is INCONCLUSIVE; a
detected effect is SUPPORTED in the declared direction and NOT SUPPORTED (opposite direction)
against it; otherwise NO DETECTABLE EFFECT if equivalent, else INCONCLUSIVE. For a blind or useless
metric the correct definite outcome is metric invalid, which a validator can reach only by showing
blindness: the oracle runs GATE 4's rule on the dataset (200 injections at the card's dose against
their shams, the upper 95% bound below δ\_min; the random-gene score of N6c facing the G2M module,
the random number of N6a); for an ambiguous metric it takes either route: the effect's outcome
where that is correct, else metric invalid where GATE 4's rule shows blindness (the fourth review
found a wrong effect outcome kept there). The constant of N6b is DEGENERATE METRIC.

A case — a condition, variant and pool pair — is **establishable** when the oracle reaches a
correct definite outcome with power ≥ 0.9 in the pilot, on 100 datasets drawn with public seeds
outside the panel's (the useless metrics of N6 depend on the pair only through its level's δ\_min
and are computed once per level). The same datasets give most of what section 6's model of a
sound validator needs: the outcomes of the effect's analysis, and for a blind, ambiguous or useless
metric the outcomes of the engine's rules; GATE 4's odds for a valid metric come from the truth's
datasets (the case's own where its data change the pair, else the background's null: above), and
GATE 0's refusals from the engine itself (section 6). **The oracle is a
reference, not a bound:** it knows what the engine has to find out, but its tests are not the
engine's — on simulated data the second review found the engine more decisive than the oracle on N2
and less on N8 — so establishability is a property of the case under the oracle's rules. The
engine's own verdicts never decide establishability, which would make decisiveness true by
selection.

Group labels are neutral (`A` / `B`); the side that receives an artifact or a signal is drawn by
the key, and so is the gene pair of every dataset, uniformly over the pool (a 62-bit draw reduced
modulo the pool's size) and independently of the condition. The metric of every norm_pearson
case is the engine's log-normalized Pearson of the dataset's pair, with the pair's negative
control and the background's positive control (GATE 5), an injected coupling of the pair at its
level's saturation dose (GATE 4), the SESOI of its level and δ\_min = 0.5 × SESOI.

### 3.3 The key, blinding and the automatic run

**The key is a public random value that does not exist when the code is frozen.** It is the
randomness of one round of **drand's quicknet chain** (League of Entropy): every 3 seconds a
threshold of independent organisations jointly signs the round number with a BLS signature
(BLS12-381, signatures on G1, RFC 9380 hashing), and the round's randomness is the sha256 of that
signature — 64 hex characters (`beacon.py`). No one can know a future round's value, no member of
the network can bias it alone, and anyone can check a round against the chain's fixed public key.
**Why drand rather than the NIST beacon:** NIST's pulse is produced and signed by a single
operator (its availability depends on one agency, and it went offline in a government shutdown);
drand's value is a threshold signature of many independent operators, served by several public
relays, and each quicknet round is verifiable on its own with one public key and no certificate
chain. The key decides every dataset's condition, variant, side, gene pair and seed, and the order
of the dataset IDs (`panel.assign`).

**How the round is fixed.** After the pilot is committed (section 8), the workflow pushes the
annotated tag `panel-v1-run` on that commit, the head of the run's own branch `prereg/panel-v1`; its
message names the round —
`drand quicknet round: <R>` — chosen just before the push as the first round at least 65 minutes
later, and after the push the workflow checks that the round lies at least an hour after it, by
the runner's clock and by the newest round the relays serve then, whose signature must verify
(`beacon.latest`: the beacon's own clock; the fourth review found the runner's clock the only
measure), the smaller counting (else it stops before any key exists). It then waits for the round,
fetches it from the relays
(`api.drand.sh`, `api2.drand.sh`, `api3.drand.sh`, `drand.cloudflare.com`), accepts it only if its
signature verifies and the verified answers agree, writes the key record (round, signature,
randomness, every relay's answer) to `key.json` — printed in the workflow's log and committed with
the results; the manifest keeps the round and its value — and runs the panel. **From the moment the
key exists no one decides anything:** the backgrounds, the pilot, the drops and the code are fixed
in commits that precede the round, the run is automatic, and every number afterwards comes from the
frozen code.

**Checking the key, by anyone.** `curl https://api.drand.sh/52db9ba70e0cc0f6eaf7803dd07447a1f5477735fd3f661792ba94600c84e971/public/<R>`
(or any relay) returns the round's signature and randomness;
`python validation/prereg/beacon.py verify --round <R> --signature <hex>` checks the signature
against the public key in `beacon.py` (also served at `…/info` with the chain's genesis time and
period) and prints the randomness, which must equal the `key` in `key.json` and `manifest.json`.
The tag's push time is in the repository's event log and the workflow run's log; the round's time
is genesis + (R − 1) × 3 s.

**Blinding.** The claim cards of the norm_pearson conditions on B1 are identical across
conditions except for the ID and the direction (`test_claim_cards_do_not_reveal_the_condition`):
the pair, its level's SESOI and δ\_min, the injection's dose and its controls follow the pair,
which the key draws independently of the condition. Claims are directional: an effect's card
declares its true direction (the sign of Δ\*); a null's card a direction drawn by the key. The
blinding is partial and is not the main protection: N4 (another card and design), N5 (a paired
design), N6 (another metric) and N7 (another background) are structurally distinguishable. The
main protection is that the engine, the panel, the pilot and the drops are fixed before the key
exists, and that the run is automatic, once per card, with no exclusions. The truth is generated by
`panel.py`'s own binomial thinning (as in seqgendiff, Gerard 2020).

**Determinism.** The run is a deterministic function of the frozen tag, the backgrounds, pilot.json
(whose drops follow the timing pilot's measured speed of the runners, section 4) and the beacon's
value: every dataset is built from the key's entry, the engine's seed comes from the card, and each
worker uses one BLAS thread and one numerical path — OpenBLAS's Haswell kernels, numpy's dispatch
up to AVX2 — which every script that runs cards sets itself before numpy loads
(`frozen.NUMERIC_ENV`, unless its caller set them; the workflow sets the same and a fixed hash
seed; `test_runner_pins_one_blas_thread_per_worker_before_numpy_loads`). A report holds only what
the engine computed — the time, the machine, the worker's peak memory and the run log go to
`runtime.json` — so on one kind of machine the same card gives the same bytes with any number of
workers and any hash seed (`test_the_same_cards_give_byte_identical_reports`: 20 cards run twice,
in fresh processes with different hash seeds and workers), and anyone can rebuild every dataset from
the key and check its sha256 in the manifest. **Between CPU models the last digits of a report's
numbers can differ**, never its verdict or cause: the first review found differences in the last
digits before the kernel variables were set, and the second found them still on one shard of a dry
run re-run on an AVX-512 Xeon (relative differences up to 8.4e-13, the same verdicts and causes —
the review's measurement, quoted as such: no script here produces that number),
from libraries the variables do not reach. A re-run therefore gives the same verdicts, causes and
numbers up to the last digits; `blind.py verify` compares reports that way (below).

**Infrastructure and engine errors.** A shard that leaves no results (a lost runner, a timeout, a
worker killed for memory) is run again from the same inputs by the workflow's `rerun` job, inside
the same attempt, which gives the same verdicts; the re-run is recorded with its reason in
`runtime.json` and the results' summary. An exception of the engine on a card is a result (an
error), never re-run; so is an exception of the panel building a dataset, on each of its cards
(the fourth review: it stopped the whole shard, and the re-run the same way).

**One attempt** (the third review found it enforced only at the start: the run tag could be deleted
and its job re-run for a new round, and `collect` force-pushed the results). The real run is one
attempt of one workflow run: every job of it stops if the run is a re-run (`github.run_attempt` > 1;
`guard` checks it, and every later job again in the frozen tag's `frozen.py`, since a re-run of
failed jobs does not re-run `guard`); `collect` creates `results/panel-v1` with a plain push, which
fails if the branch exists (the dry run's scratch results branch alone is replaced); every shard
records its workflow run and attempt, and the results' summary lists them. A real run that fails
before the run tag is pushed has named no round and no key exists: starting it again — re-pushing
the frozen tag after deleting the run's own branch — is a decision recorded in `DEVIATIONS.md`. A
real run that fails after it is reported as failed, with the reason; it is not run again.

**Pull requests** that touch the panel run the same workflow as a **dry run**, with the same jobs
and scripts: the Census selection as a rehearsal that prints no identities (with a 100-cell test
extraction) and the B3 and B4 checks; simulated backgrounds written as the selection's files, with a
`backgrounds.json` that also names B3 (`simulate.py write`); a scratch data branch from the pull
request's head; the whole pilot as a smoke test — `oracle.py --smoke` (the same code with tiny
sizes; its base, two parts and their merge), the timing pilot with its drops on one dataset of every
condition, `oc.py --pilot` — committed
to the scratch branch; a scratch run tag pushed and deleted; a drand round two minutes ahead fetched
and verified as the key; the backgrounds prepared from their files; two shards of four datasets
(shard 1 lost on purpose and re-run) with the smoke pilot; collection, scoring, a commit to
`results/panel-v1-dry`; the scratch branch deleted. (The first review found that the pilot had never
run through its command lines before the tag, and that the anchors' B3 in `backgrounds.json`
stopped it; `test_the_pilot_and_the_run_go_through_their_command_lines_on_files` runs the same
chain.) In the dry run of workflow run 37817228721 the four relays served the chain information of
`beacon.py`, round 32892512 verified on all four and its randomness became the key (the round is
now an offline test of the constants, `test_the_chain_constants_verify_a_real_quicknet_round`), and
the eight reports of the two shards were byte-identical to a re-run of the same shards in the
agent's container, another machine with half the workers; in the dry run of workflow run
37842705951 the second review found one shard's four reports different in their last digits on an
AVX-512 Xeon (above). **Anyone can check a finished run the same way, from the results branch
alone:** it holds the key record, the pilot, the manifest, the reports and the planned backgrounds
the shards read (`compact.tar`), so neither the workflow's artifacts (kept 90 days) nor a new
download from the Census is needed; `blind.py verify` re-runs the first N datasets of the key's
order into an empty directory, checks every dataset's sha256 against the manifest, and compares
every report with the published one (bound to the manifest by its sha256): byte-identical, or the
same — the verdict, the cause and every field equal, numbers within a relative 10⁻⁹
(`blind.same_report`) —, else different
(`test_verify_re_runs_a_finished_run_and_compares_its_reports`). Anchors R1–R3 are not blinded
(their truth is public); the engine is frozen before they are run.

### 3.4 The engine rules this panel depends on (frozen with the tag)

- **GATE 4** (decided 2026-10-08): the coupling is injected 200 times, each against its sham, and
  the mean response gets its two-sided 95% t interval. PASS: the lower bound in the declared
  direction is above 0. FAIL ("metric invalid"): the upper bound is below δ\_min (pre-registered;
  default 0.5 × SESOI) — a proof of blindness, not an absence of response; an interval inside
  (0, δ\_min) is FAIL. Otherwise UNTESTED. Where GATE 4 ran and is UNTESTED, a positive control on
  another pair does not make the metric valid for the claim.
- **GATE 5** (decided 2026-10-08 on the owner's condition, probe p15): a silent positive control
  FAILs only where the metric is shown blind to a coupling of dose 2.0 injected into the control's
  genes (the same rule, at α/K); the negative control fails at α/K as before.
- Probe p14 (simulated backgrounds of the panel's size, δ\_min 0.05, the saturation doses): GATE 4
  PASSes the high level in 40/40 datasets, never FAILs the medium level (0/40) and FAILs the low
  level, where log-normalized Pearson is blind, in 39/40 (`p14_gate4_by_expression_level.log`).
- **The SESOI's scale and "explained by depth"** (after the first review; dev-set journal D7): the
  cards declare `sesoi_scale: observed`, because the pilot fixes the SESOI on the metric's observed
  scale, so the engine's TOST and power check use ±SESOI as the oracle does (by default the engine
  attenuates a construct-scale SESOI by GATE 0's λ); and with a SESOI, "explained by depth" also
  needs the corrected effect shown smaller than the SESOI by the TOST, else the effect is
  INCONCLUSIVE.

## 4. Replicates, precision and compute

**Replicates.** The success criteria follow one principle (section 6). S1 is a conjunction over
the five key null conditions (N1, N2 at c = 0.5, N5, N6c, N8), so the principle applies to S1 as a
whole: a sound validator passes all five with probability ≥ 0.90, while a doubled error in any one
condition passes that condition with probability ≤ 0.05. With the nominal false SUPPORTED rate
α/2 = 0.025 that needs 790 datasets per key condition with at most 29 false SUPPORTED each
(P(all five pass | sound) = 0.915; P(a condition passes | doubled) = 0.046; `oc.log`). Because the
threshold moves in integer steps, the rule first holds at 763 datasets and fails again at 769–785
and 802–808; 790 meets it. Other conditions keep 50–790 datasets: they enter the criteria's
cells, the dose–verdict curves and P4.

**Shared donors** (the first and third reviews). Every dataset is an independent draw from its
fixed background (its own seed from the key), so given the background the outcomes of a
condition's datasets are independent, the counts of errors binomial, and the probabilities above
exact for the rates on these backgrounds (the third review: the earlier text called the counts
overdispersed). The datasets do share donors, and their outcomes can depend on which donors a
dataset draws; that does not overdisperse the counts here, but it bounds how far a rate measured
on one background's donors carries over to other donors of the same kind. Read that way — the
background's donors as a random sample — a design effect of 1.1, 1.2 or 1.5 (a beta-binomial with
the binomial's mean) would let a sound validator pass S1 as a whole with probability 0.892, 0.868
or 0.800 and a doubled error pass a condition with 0.054, 0.062 or 0.085; the principle holds up to
a design effect of 1.044 (`oc.log`). The owner's decision of the third round stands (item 5): the
design effect of every primary criterion is reported with its shared-donor interval, and above 1.5
it is a pre-registered limitation on reading the rate beyond the background's donors; the results
also state S1's operating characteristics at the realized design effects (`score.py`,
`s1_at_realized_deff`).

**Timing pilot** (`timing.py` → `timing.log`): on simulated backgrounds of the panel's size (an N1
dataset has 3,200 cells × 2,000 genes), 26 claim cards of 24 datasets — one of every condition, the
rest drawn in proportion to the conditions' cards (stratified after the first review: the earlier
sample had no E1–E3) — took 28.9 s per card on one core (median 26.3 s; N6c, whose GATE 4 injects
the G2M module, 83 s) and 29.5 s per card in each of 4 parallel workers, with no engine error and at
most 799 MB per worker: every card of the panel at its condition's mean time is 62.4 CPU-hours, and
a shard of 4 workers is expected to take 0.78 h, inside the 3-hour budget; 4 workers at their peak
(736 MB each) use 2,944 MB of the 16,095 MB here (re-measured at the code of the fourth review's
fixes, with N4, N6a and N7 at 300 datasets; the third review had found the earlier log older than
the engine's last change). The pilot step repeats the measurement on the real backgrounds
(`timing_pilot.log`) and decides the drops and checks the memory by the rules below; at this speed
its 300 cards for GATE 0's refusal shares (section 6) take about 37 minutes at 4 workers.

**Compute plan.** The blind run takes 20 shard jobs of 335 claim cards, four workers each, on
GitHub-hosted runners (this repository is public: free, 4 cores and 16 GB per standard Linux
runner, 6 hours per job, 20 jobs at once). **Drop rule** (mechanical, `timing.py --write-drops` in
the pilot step, on the real backgrounds and the run's 4 workers): if a shard's expected time — every
card of the design at its condition's mean time per card at 4 workers, over 20 shards of 4 workers
(the key's order mixes the conditions over the shards, and the shard job's 330-minute timeout leaves
a slow shard 1.8 times the budget) — exceeds 3 hours, conditions are dropped, never replicates of the remaining
ones, in this order until it fits: the N3 steps; the E1 doses other
than the base dose; the N2 steps other than c = 0.5; N4. The key null conditions, E1–E3 at the
base dose and N7 (at most 24 mice per dataset) are never dropped: if a shard's expected time still
exceeds the budget after every drop, the pilot stops the run before the key exists
(`timing.decide_drops`). The drops are written to `pilot.json` (`"dropped"`, which `panel.assign`
accepts only as a prefix of this order, after a background's drop, section 3.1) before the key
exists. **Memory rule** (the third review: the drops weighed time only, and a worker killed for
memory after the key would have left no results): the timing pilot's peak memory of one worker
(its sample holds every condition) times the run's 4 workers must fit in 75% of the runner's
memory, else the pilot stops the run before the key exists (`timing.check_memory`); and a worker
the system kills breaks its shard at once (`run_panel.fork_map`) rather than at the job's timeout,
so the shard is lost and re-run (section 3.3).

## 5. Primary outcomes

Every rate is reported as k/n with its two-sided 95% Clopper-Pearson interval, per condition, per
expression level and per truth class, and pooled (`score.py`).

- **P1 False SUPPORTED** on every card where SUPPORTED is not an allowed outcome (null data,
  useless or blind metrics). With directional claims the engine's two-sided test at α gives α/2 on
  a null with a valid metric (`test_a_directional_claim_is_tested_two_sided_at_alpha`).
- **P2 Outside the allowed outcomes**, all claim cards, pooled. A crash (a missing report or an
  engine exception) and a label that does not match its cause count as outside. Reported; the
  criteria count each kind of error in its own cell (section 6, the third review).
- **P3 Correct definite outcomes** on establishable cards: definite and allowed. The share of any
  definite outcome is also reported.
- **P4 False "metric invalid"** (GATE 4 or 5) on the cards whose metric is valid by the truth.
- **P5 Per-gate sensitivity and specificity**: GATE 1 on the N2/N3 steps and N1; "explained by
  depth" on N2 at c ≤ 0.5 per level; GATE 4 PASS / FAIL / UNTESTED per truth class and level; GATE
  5's negative control on N1; GATE 0's refusals; the effect retained on E1–E3.
- **P6 False NO DETECTABLE EFFECT** on every card where it is not allowed (a blind or useless metric;
  a real effect at or above the SESOI).
- **P7 Rule violations and crashes**, on all claim cards, apart (the owner's fifth round): a verdict
  the engine's deterministic rules cannot give there (a label that does not match its cause, a cause
  the panel does not expect, UNIDENTIFIABLE, DEGENERATE METRIC on a metric that varies, an effect
  verdict where the rules give none); and the crashes, a missing report or an exception of the
  engine, each listed with its traceback in `scores.json` ("crashes").
- **Dose–verdict curves** (E1 doses, N2 and N3 steps): share of each outcome with its interval.

**The design effect, reported for every primary criterion** (fixed now). Given the background the
datasets are independent draws and the primary intervals exact; but datasets of a condition share
donors, and their outcomes can depend on the donors drawn, which bounds how far a rate carries to
other donors (section 4, shared donors). Working model: the covariance of two datasets' outcomes,
given the donors they drew, grows with the number they share, Cov(y_j, y_k) = β·|S_j ∩ S_k|; β is the moment
estimate over all pairs of datasets, truncated at 0; the variance of the rate is
(Σ residual² + β Σ overlaps) / n², its ratio to the binomial variance the design effect, and the
interval Clopper-Pearson at the effective sample size n / deff (Korn and Graubard 1998). These
intervals are secondary; **where a criterion's design effect exceeds 1.5, that is reported in the
results as a pre-registered limitation** on reading the rate beyond the background's donors, not a
failure of the criterion (`score.py`, `limitations`). On simulated data the
interval stays at the binomial one without donor effects and covers where the binomial interval
does not with them (`test_the_shared_donor_interval_widens_only_when_donors_drive_the_outcome`).

## 6. Success criteria

**Principle** (decided 2026-10-08): every criterion passes a sound validator with probability
≥ 0.90 and a validator whose error is double the nominal with probability ≤ 0.05; S1, a conjunction
over five conditions, meets it as a whole; and the criteria together pass a sound validator with
probability ≥ 0.90 (the third round). For each criterion the threshold is the most lenient one at
which the doubled-error validator still passes with probability ≤ 0.05; "at most k\* errors of n"
is the same as "upper 95% Clopper-Pearson bound ≤ T".

**Nominal errors, by cause** (`oc.py`). The routes to a wrong outcome have the sizes the rules
are designed to: a false SUPPORTED α/2 = 0.025 (the effect test is two-sided at α, the direction
checked afterwards); a false "metric invalid" on a valid metric 2α = 0.10 (GATE 4's upper bound below
δ\_min α/2, GATE 5's negative control α, a silent positive control shown blind α/2); a false NO
DETECTABLE EFFECT or "explained by depth" α = 0.05 (the TOST's size at the SESOI); NOT SUPPORTED
against the direction, where that is an error, α/2 (one tail). **A card's rates are those of a sound validator on
its case** (`oc.sound_model`, decided after the first review, which found routes to an error that
the designed sizes miss; completed after the second): one that follows the engine's pre-registered
rules correctly, in the engine's verdict order (`report.decide_cause`) — GATE 5's failure, then GATE
4's, is "metric invalid"; then an effect explained by depth, which the engine checks before it asks
for the metric's validity; then the effect's outcome where GATE 4 PASSed, INCONCLUSIVE where it was
UNTESTED (`oracle.engine_outcome`, tested against `decide_cause`). For a valid metric, GATE 5 FAILs
with its designed 1.5α — the negative controls are chosen so that it does (section 3.1; the third
review found the controls chosen by partial correlation alone making GATE 5 fail some pairs' null
datasets far more often, and a test now checks the engine's GATE 5 on the panel's null datasets with
its controls) — and GATE 4 with the odds the pilot measured on the case's response (a normal model
of the case's datasets); after a PASS the effect's analysis decides as the oracle's did on the
case's datasets, after an UNTESTED its share of "explained by depth" stays. For a blind or ambiguous
metric GATE 5 FAILs with 1.5α too ("metric invalid", allowed there), and otherwise the engine's
order decides as the oracle found it on the case's datasets, where it ran GATE 4's rule; for a
useless metric the engine's order alone; the constant is DEGENERATE METRIC. **GATE 0 decides
first** (after the constant's DEGENERATE METRIC): its refusal is allowed everywhere and never an
error, so it only lowers decisiveness, and the model refuses with the share of refusals the frozen
engine itself gave, before the key, on establishable cards of the card's S3 stratum — 100 per
stratum on the real backgrounds, datasets of a public key that the panel's key does not draw
(`timing.py --refusals`, written to pilot.json's "gate0_refusals"; the fourth review found a sound
engine failing S3 at a refusal share of 15% that the model left out). The oracle has no copy of
GATE 0, so this is the one input of the model measured with the engine, and it is reported before
the key (`timing_pilot.log`, `oc_pilot.log`). **On the strata with a valid metric (real effects;
nulls) the model takes it at most 0.20, a usefulness bound set in advance** (the owner's fifth
round: a GATE 0 refusing on half of them halved d̄ and still passed S3): **a stratum on which the
frozen engine refused more often before the key fails S3** — the tool is silent too often on a good
metric —, which `oc_pilot.log` shows and pilot.json records before the key ("s3_rules",
"refusal_bound_failed"); the run goes on, its other measurements are needed (`oc.GATE0_BOUND`,
`oc.refusal_bound_failures`). On the blind and useless metrics the measured share stands: there a
refusal protects from a false conclusion. A copy of GATE 0 in the oracle is planned for v2
(section 10). From the model, per card: the probability of each wrong outcome where it is wrong
— a false SUPPORTED, a false "metric invalid", a false NO DETECTABLE EFFECT, NOT SUPPORTED against
the direction, explained by depth —, on null data, where it is allowed, of NOT SUPPORTED against
the direction, of any outcome outside the allowed set, and of a correct definite outcome. The oracle's
analysis of the effect is the engine's: on data without a planted nuisance it equalizes depth as
the engine does for the composition estimand (one common thinning ratio, five equalizations, the
replicate values averaged; the third review found it analysing N1, N5 and N7 uncorrected), and only
where a nuisance is planted does it apply the true correction instead. Where
the pilot measured nothing (the scenarios below, N4) the designed sizes of the routes the card's
allowed outcomes leave open (e.g. 0.175 for a valid metric on null data without a planted artifact,
0.125 with one, 0.15 for a blind metric, 0.225 for a real effect) and decisiveness 0.85, or what the
errors leave of it.

**The thresholds use, per card, the larger of the sound validator's rate and the error's designed
size** (`oc.rule_nominals`; the second review found that a measured rate far below the design leaves
too few expected errors to tell a doubled rate apart — at 0.001 on 5,600 cards the sound validator
passed with probability 0.51): the rules are designed to allow the designed size, and the validation
asks no more of them. Where even that leaves the principle unattainable on a criterion's n, its
nominal is raised to the first rate of the grid nominal × 1.01ʲ at which it holds
(`oc.error_criterion`; up to 0.5, else the criterion is reported only).

**Every error criterion is a set of cells** (the third review: errors pooled over unlike cards were
absorbed — a validator that said SUPPORTED on half of N4's cards without the replicate unit, or on a
quarter of N7's, passed the pooled S2, and one that said NOT SUPPORTED against the direction, or NO
DETECTABLE EFFECT, wherever a sound validator is unsure passed the pooled S4). Each kind of error is
counted in its own cell — S2 per group of conditions, S4, S5 and S6 per stratum of the data, S4 also
per cause — and **every cell must pass**. A cell's nominal is its cards' mean rule nominal, raised
where needed until the principle holds on its n **and** a sound validator, at the cards' mean rate,
fails the cell with probability ≤ 0.0005 (`oc.error_criterion`, `oc.EPS_CELL`): the sixteen cells
together then cost the joint requirement less than 1%, which S1 at its design leaves room for.
P2, every outcome outside the allowed set pooled over the cards, is reported, not a criterion: each
wrong outcome is in exactly one cell of S1, S2 or S4–S6, or in S7a or S7b (`oc.error_cells`). **NOT SUPPORTED
against the direction on null data**, allowed there (the claim is false), is a detection in the
other tail all the same: its rate has its own cell, S4 on the nulls, at α/2 or the measured rate
(the fourth review: a validator that said it on 16% of the null cards passed every criterion).
**A cell's resolution** — the error rate it fails with probability ≥ 0.95, about twice its raised
nominal — is printed for every cell in `oc.log` and `oc_pilot.log`; a validator whose error stays
below it is not one the cell can tell apart (the fourth review: on S2's groups of 100 to 200 cards
a false SUPPORTED on 6% to 12% of one group passed). Every group of S2 of the null conditions
therefore has 790 cards (the owner's fifth round; at the fourth review's 300 a group resolved 8.8%,
at 790 it resolves 6.0%); the real effects' group has the blind cards among E1–E3, as many as
the truth makes them.
