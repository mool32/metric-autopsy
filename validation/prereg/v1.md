# Pre-registration v1 — confirmatory validation of metric-autopsy v0.3

**Status: FINAL DRAFT, second round, for approval by the project owner (2026-10-08).** It
incorporates the owner's decisions of 2026-10-08, both rounds (section 10). Nothing is frozen
until the owner approves this file after an independent review of the code in a separate
session. On approval: the engine, this file and the code of section 2 are frozen under the
annotated tag `v0.3.0-prereg`, whose message carries the key's commitment and the sha256 of
every frozen file (also recorded in `PROJECT.md`). After the tag, the engine and the protocol
change only through `validation/prereg/DEVIATIONS.md` (D-series, one entry per change: what, why,
when, and which results it touches). Every number cited from the validation comes from the
scripts named here, with the hashes from their JSON reports.

Scope: RNA only (scRNA-seq counts). Other modalities are out of v1.

---

## 1. What the validation tests

The claim under test is about the validator, not about any biology:

> On datasets whose truth is known by construction or by design, metric-autopsy v0.3 (frozen)
> (a) rarely certifies a claim that is false — false SUPPORTED on nulls, artifacts and useless
> metrics; (b) rarely gives a verdict that is wrong *given the design* — outside the set of
> verdicts that are correct for that design; and (c) commits to a correct definite verdict where
> the design makes the truth establishable, instead of retreating to INCONCLUSIVE.

(c) exists because a validator that never errs because it never commits is useless. Both axes are
primary.

**Scope of inference.** The rates generalize to repeated samples (donors and cells) from the two
backgrounds B1 and B2; to the artifacts of the binomial-thinning family (capture loss, dropout)
plus N8's per-cell variable capture, which lies outside the engine's correction family; and to one
valid metric, log-normalized Pearson of a gene pair, at three expression levels (with two useless
metrics, N6). Other metrics — `mi_3bin` included —, other backgrounds and other artifacts are
outside v1.

The development set (`validation/probes/`) found the bugs and the fixes were developed against
it. It plays no part here. The panel uses the same mechanisms as the development set (binomial
thinning, dropout, an injected coupling); what differs is the code (written separately; it never
imports the engine), the backgrounds (real data) and the blind assignment of cases.

## 3. The panel

### 3.1 Backgrounds and the pair pool

Each background is chosen **mechanically** by the criteria below: the agent lists the candidates
that meet every criterion, with the facts that decide each, applies the choice rule, downloads
the file, and records in `backgrounds.json` the source URL, version, file name, sha256, cell filter,
donor column, counts source and gene-symbol column, committed before any panel dataset exists. A
background for which no candidate qualifies is dropped with the cases that need it; the drop is
recorded (after the tag, as a D-series entry). Nothing is substituted by judgment.

| ID | Use | Criteria (all required) | Choice rule | Candidate |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| B1 | nulls and injected signals N1–N6, N8, E1–E3 | droplet 3' platform (10x Chromium); one annotated cell type with ≥ 200 cells in each of ≥ 24 donors; raw UMI counts; donor IDs in the metadata; public download without registration | most donors meeting the cell threshold; ties: more cells per donor | human skin fibroblasts, CELLxGENE (`DATASETS.md`, 179 donors) |
| B2 | mouse-level null N7, sex anchor R1 | droplet 3' platform; one tissue and cell type with ≥ 12 mice of ≥ 200 cells each, both sexes; raw counts; mouse IDs; public | the tissue with the most such mice; ties: more cells per mouse | Tabula Muris Senis, droplet |
| B3 | cell-cycle and content anchors R2 | plate-based capture with ERCC spike-ins; phase assigned by an independent measurement (DNA content sort), not from the transcriptome; ≥ 50 cells per phase; raw counts including ERCC; public | most cells per phase | Buettner et al. 2015, mESC G1 / S / G2M (E-MTAB-2805) |
| B4 | FUCCI anchor R3 | FUCCI reporter intensities for every cell (phase labels independent of the transcriptome); full-length scRNA-seq with raw counts; ≥ 300 cells; plate IDs; public | most cells with FUCCI intensities; ties: more plates | Mahdessian et al. 2021, U2OS FUCCI, Smart-seq2 |

**The pair pool**, fixed before the key (`panel.plan_background`; public seed 20261008):
- Only donors with ≥ 200 cells are used. A gene is eligible for a pair if it is not mitochondrial
  or ribosomal and is detected in ≥ 5% of the cells of every donor (which excludes sex- and
  donor-specific genes).
- **Levels** by mean count per cell and detection: *low* — detected in a minority of cells
  (10% to 50%); *medium* — detected in a majority, mean < 2; *high* — detected in a majority,
  mean ≥ 2. In each level the 1,000 most expressed eligible genes enter the search.
- A pair's correlation is the pooled within-donor correlation of log-normalized expression over
  all cells with the log library size partialled out (what two genes share beyond depth), on 200
  cells per donor. Per level the **4 most correlated disjoint pairs** form the pool (12 pairs per
  background). The correlation of log-normalized expression on co-detected cells — the metric
  itself — was tried first and rejected: at the low level it is set by the cells' totals, so on
  simulated data it ranked the rarest genes, not the coupled ones.
- **Controls.** Each pool pair has its own negative control: the unused pair of its level closest
  to it in mean expression whose partial correlation is below 0.02 in absolute value. The positive
  control (GATE 5) is one pair per background, the next most correlated disjoint pair of the high
  level, as a housekeeping pair would be.
- The kept genes are the named genes and then the most expressed, 2,000 in all; N6c's 50 random
  genes are drawn from the kept genes; the G2M genes present are kept.
- Within a dataset donors (mice) are drawn without replacement, 200 cells each; across datasets
  they are reused (B1 has 179 donors, so at most about 11 datasets of a condition are
  donor-disjoint); section 5 fixes how shared donors enter the intervals.

The plan is computed from exact integer sums, with correlations rounded to 10 decimals before
ranking, so a dense and a sparse copy of the same counts give the same pool (tested).

### 3.2 Cases

The **allowed set** is the set of verdicts that are correct *given the design*; anything outside
it is an error. A verdict is scored by its label, the text before " — " (SUPPORTED covers
"SUPPORTED (provisional until replicated)" and "SUPPORTED — replicated"); bracketed qualifiers
(parametric only, underpowered, non-directional) do not change the label.

**Δ\* and the allowed sets of the real effects.** For every pool pair the pilot computes Δ\*, the
population difference of the metric under the injected coupling in the version of the condition
without its artifact — E1 at each of its doses; E2 and E3 as E1 at the key dose — as the mean of
paired draws (one donor's 200 cells with the coupling and with the sham, 2,000 draws per pair and
dose), with its standard error; both are written to `pilot.json` before the key. Where
|Δ\*| < SESOI of the pair's level, NO DETECTABLE EFFECT is also correct; where |Δ\*| ≥ SESOI it is an
error, in E2 and E3 too, where the attenuation by the capture loss should make the engine report
UNDERPOWERED (an INCONCLUSIVE verdict), not NO DETECTABLE EFFECT. The card's direction is the sign
of Δ\* (the signal side is the higher one where Δ\* > 0).

**The oracle** (`oracle.py`) is told what the engine has to find out. It takes the metric's
validity as known, removes a planted nuisance by applying it to the other side as well (capture
loss with the true factor, dropout with the true fraction, N8's per-cell capture with fresh
Beta(2, 2) draws), and tests the per-donor metric values with the test the graded replicate rule
prescribes (exact permutation over donors at ≥ 4 per group, Welch at 3; sign flips for the paired
N5; equivalence by TOST, the (1 − 2α) interval inside ±SESOI), at α = 0.05 two-sided. Its verdict
follows the engine's order: a detected raw difference that the true correction removes (less than
half retained) is NOT SUPPORTED (explained); one whose sign the correction reverses is
INCONCLUSIVE; a detected effect is SUPPORTED in the declared direction and NOT SUPPORTED against
it; otherwise NO DETECTABLE EFFECT if equivalent, else INCONCLUSIVE. For a useless metric (N6)
the oracle is the valid metric facing the same injected signal (log-normalized Pearson for the
coupling, a G2M score for the module), which must respond by more than 4 standard errors (then
NOT SUPPORTED); a constant is DEGENERATE METRIC.

A case — a condition, variant and level — is **establishable** when the oracle reaches *any
correct definite verdict* (in the allowed set, not INCONCLUSIVE) with power ≥ 0.9 in the pilot,
on 100 datasets drawn with public seeds outside the panel's; the engine's correct definite verdict
is then expected. The oracle's power is the most a validator could reach; the engine's own
verdicts never decide establishability, which would make decisiveness true by selection.

**Per level, from the pilot** (before the key): the SESOI — the smallest value of the grid
0.005–1.0 at which the oracle's TOST establishes N1 with power ≥ 0.9 (used for B2's pairs of the
same level too); the key dose — the smallest dose of the grid 0.25–4.0 at which the oracle detects
E1 in the direction of Δ\* with power ≥ 0.9 **and** the level's Δ\* (the mean over its four pairs)
is ≥ 1.25 × SESOI; where no dose meets both, the level uses the largest grid dose and has no key
dose (its E cases are then rarely establishable). The pilot also measures, per level, the oracle's
power to detect N2's raw difference at c = 0.5: N2 and N3 test "explained by depth" only on a
level where it is ≥ 0.9; if no level reaches it, `pilot.json` records before the key that N2 and
N3 are uninformative (they stay in the panel as nulls with an artifact).

Group labels are neutral (`A` / `B`); the side that receives an artifact or a signal is drawn by
the key, and so is the gene pair of every dataset, uniformly over the pool and independently of
the condition. The metric of every norm_pearson case is the engine's log-normalized Pearson of the
dataset's pair, with the pair's negative control and the background's positive control (GATE 5)
and an injected coupling of the pair at the engine's default strength (GATE 4). A depth bias of
this metric does not block where the declared composition correction removes depth between groups
(decided 2026-10-08, probe p13).

### 3.3 The key, blinding and the blind run

**The key** is 128 bits written as 32 lowercase hex characters (`secrets.token_hex(16)`). The
project owner generates it at approval, stores it as the repository secret `KEY_SEED`, and
publishes its **commitment** — the sha256 of the hex string in UTF-8 (`panel.key_commitment`) —
in a comment on the pull request and in the message of the tag `v0.3.0-prereg` (a line
`key sha256: <64 hex>`). The key decides every dataset's condition, variant, side, gene pair and
seed, and the order of the dataset IDs (`panel.assign`). It never enters the repository, a log or
this session before scoring.

**Blinding.** The claim cards of the norm_pearson conditions are identical across conditions
except for the ID and the direction (`test_claim_cards_do_not_reveal_the_condition`): the pair,
its level's SESOI and its controls follow the pair, which the key draws independently of the
condition. Claims are directional: an effect's card declares its true direction (the sign of
Δ\*); a null's card a direction drawn by the key. The blinding is partial and is not the main
protection: N4 (another card and design), N5 (a paired design), N6 (another metric) and N7
(another background) are structurally distinguishable, so blinding hides only the differences
within N1–N3, N8 and E1–E3. The main protection is the frozen engine, run once per card, with no
exclusions and no re-runs. The truth is generated by `panel.py`'s own binomial thinning (as in
seqgendiff, Gerard 2020).

**The blind run** (`.github/workflows/panel.yml`, `blind.py`) builds the datasets on the fly and
stores none of them:
1. The owner pushes the tag `panel-v1-run` on the commit that adds `backgrounds.json` and
   `pilot.json`. The workflow refuses to run unless that commit descends from `v0.3.0-prereg` and
   its engine, panel code, pinned requirements and workflow are byte-identical to the tag's
   (`blind.py guard`); it runs with read-only repository permissions and writes nothing to the
   repository.
2. One job downloads the backgrounds named in `backgrounds.json`, checks their sha256, plans them
   and passes the compacted backgrounds to the shards with their sha256; each shard checks them
   and their content against the backgrounds the pilot used (`pilot.json`).
3. Twenty shard jobs (a matrix; shard k takes the datasets k, k + 20, … of the key's order) read
   `KEY_SEED`, check it against the commitment in the tag's message, build their datasets, run the
   engine on every claim card with one worker per core, and save the reports, the run log, a
   manifest with the canonical sha256 of every dataset (`panel.dataset_sha256`) and claim card
   (`panel.card_sha256`) and the donors of every dataset, and the key's sha256. A shard that dies
   before writing its results (runner loss, timeout) is re-run once from the same tag; that is
   recorded.
4. A collection job merges the shards — same key, backgrounds and pilot; every dataset once —,
   writes `SHA256SUMS` of every file and uploads the results as the workflow's artifact.
5. **Before** the key is revealed, the agent commits the results with the artifact's sha256
   digest (GitHub reports it for every artifact) in the commit message. Downloading the artifact
   needs access to GitHub's artifact storage (`*.blob.core.windows.net`), which the agent's
   container does not have under its present network policy; without it the owner downloads the
   artifact and the agent commits it, and the digest, readable through the API, is committed
   first. After the reveal anyone can rebuild every dataset from the key and check its sha256.

Pull requests that touch the panel run the same workflow as a **dry run**: simulated backgrounds,
a public test key, two shards of four datasets; the secret is never referenced. If Actions cannot
run the panel, the fallback is one command on the owner's machine, with the key in the
environment: `python validation/prereg/blind.py all --backgrounds … --pilot … --commitment-tag
v0.3.0-prereg --out results` (the same code: prepare, one shard with every dataset, collect).
Anchors R1–R3 are not blinded (their truth is public); the engine is frozen before they are run.

## 4. Replicates, precision and compute

**Replicates.** The success criteria follow one principle (section 6). S1 is a conjunction over
the five key null conditions (N1, N2 at c = 0.5, N5, N6c, N8), so the principle applies to S1 as a
whole: a sound validator passes all five with probability ≥ 0.90, while a doubled error in any one
condition passes that condition with probability ≤ 0.05. With the nominal false SUPPORTED rate
α/2 = 0.025 that needs 790 datasets per key condition with at most 29 false SUPPORTED each
(P(all five pass | sound) = 0.915; P(a condition passes | doubled) = 0.047; `oc.log`). The 600
of the first draft would pass all five with probability 0.66 only. Because the threshold moves in
integer steps, the rule first holds at 763 datasets and fails again at 769–785 and 802–808; 790
meets it. Other conditions keep 50–200 datasets: they enter the pooled criteria, the dose–verdict
curves and P4.

**Timing pilot** (`timing.py` → `timing.log`, panel code and engine at the commit recorded there,
simulated backgrounds of the planned sizes, a sample of 24 cards with the panel's mix of
conditions and levels, on the agent's otherwise idle 4-core container, one BLAS thread per
worker): 32.5 s per claim card on one core (N5 15 s, the other conditions 29–43 s), no engine
errors; four workers ran the same cards 3.9 times faster (8.4 s of wall time per card). On GitHub's
runners, in the workflow's dry run on the pull request (run 37776941876, shard 0: 4 cores, one
BLAS thread per worker, 4 workers, 4 cards): 25.3 s per card.

**Compute plan.** The 6,000 cards take about 54 CPU-hours at the container's rate (14 hours of
wall time with its 4 workers) and about 42 at the runners' rate. On GitHub Actions (this
repository is public: GitHub-hosted runners are free with no minute limit; a standard Linux
runner has 4 cores and 16 GB of memory, a job may run 6 hours, and the free plan runs up to 20 jobs
at once — GitHub's documented limits, the cores confirmed by the dry run): 20 shards of 300 claim
cards, four workers each, about 32 minutes of computing per shard, about 40 minutes for the
whole panel when the 20 jobs run at once. Budget: 72 hours of wall time on the machine that runs
the panel; per job, at most 5.5 hours (the workflow's timeout), ten times the projection. The
pilot (section 8, step 3) re-times 24 cards on the real B1 before the key is used. If the
projection exceeds the budget, conditions are dropped — never replicates of the remaining ones —
in this order until it fits: the N3 steps; the E1 doses other than the key dose; the N2 steps
other than c = 0.5; N4. The key null conditions and E1–E3 at the key dose are never dropped. A drop is written to `pilot.json` (`"dropped"`, which `panel.assign`
accepts only as a prefix of this order) before the key is used, and the n and k\* of S2–S4 are
recomputed by `oc.py`.

## 5. Primary outcomes

Every rate is reported as k/n with its two-sided 95% Clopper-Pearson interval, per condition and
pooled (`score.py`).

- **P1 False SUPPORTED** on nulls, artifacts and useless metrics (N1–N8): share of verdicts
  labelled SUPPORTED. With directional claims the engine's two-sided test at α gives α/2 on a null
  with a valid metric (`test_a_directional_claim_is_tested_two_sided_at_alpha`).
- **P2 Outside the allowed set**, all claim cards (N and E). A missing report or an engine error
  counts as outside the set.
- **P3 Correct definite verdicts** on establishable cards: share of verdicts that are definite
  (not INCONCLUSIVE) and in the allowed set. The share of any definite verdict is also reported.
- **P4 Per-gate sensitivity and specificity**:
  - GATE 1: flags the group with a capture loss (N2/N3 steps), and stays silent on N1;
  - GATE 2 / effect field: "explained by depth" on N2 at c ≤ 0.5 on the informative levels; share
    of the effect retained on E1–E3;
  - GATE 4: PASS for the valid metric on N1/E1 per level, FAIL for N6a and N6c;
  - GATE 5: FAIL rate of the negative control on N1 (specificity);
  - GATE 0: blocking biases on the valid metric (specificity); attenuation reported, never failed;
    depth biases removed by the correction, reported.
- **Dose–verdict curves** (E1 doses, N2 and N3 steps): share of each verdict with its interval.

**Secondary, fixed now:** every rate per expression level, with its interval; and intervals that
allow for datasets sharing donors (`score.overlap_interval`). Datasets of a condition share donors
(B1 has 179), so their verdicts can be correlated. The working model: the covariance of two
datasets' outcomes grows with the number of donors they share, Cov(y_j, y_k) = β·|S_j ∩ S_k|; β is
the moment estimate over all pairs of datasets, truncated at 0; the variance of the rate is
(Σ residual² + β Σ overlaps) / n², its ratio to the binomial variance the design effect, and the
interval Clopper-Pearson at the effective sample size n / deff (Korn and Graubard 1998). A plain
cluster bootstrap is not defined here, since each dataset belongs to 16 donor clusters at once. On
simulated data the interval stays at the binomial one without donor effects and covers where the
binomial interval does not with them (`test_the_shared_donor_interval_widens_only_when_donors_drive_the_outcome`).

## 6. Success criteria

**Principle** (decided 2026-10-08): every criterion passes a sound validator with probability
≥ 0.90 and a validator whose error is double the nominal with probability ≤ 0.05; S1, a conjunction
over five conditions, meets it as a whole. Nominal error: α/2 = 0.025 for a false SUPPORTED
(directional claims) and for a verdict outside the allowed set; for decisiveness, 0.15 short of
all correct definite verdicts (0.85: establishable cases have oracle power ≥ 0.90, and a validator
must also demonstrate the metric's response, which the oracle is told), doubled 0.30. For each
criterion the threshold is the most lenient one at which the doubled-error validator still passes
with probability ≤ 0.05 (`oc.py`); a rule "at most k\* errors of n" is the same as "upper 95%
Clopper-Pearson bound ≤ T" for the T in `oc.log`.

| Criterion | n | Passes if | P(pass), sound | P(pass), doubled error |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **S1** false SUPPORTED, each of N1, N2 c = 0.5, N5, N6c, N8 | 790 each | at most 29 of 790 in every one | 0.915 for all five at 2.5% | 0.047 for a condition at 5% |
| **S2** false SUPPORTED, all null cards | 5,100 | at most 229 | 1.00 at 2.5% | 0.049 at 5% |
| **S3** correct definite verdicts, establishable cards | n_est (from the pilot) | at least k\*(n_est); e.g. 725 of 1,000 (lower bound ≥ 69.6%) | 1.00 at 85% | ≤ 0.05 at 70% |
| **S4** outside the allowed set, all cards | 6,000 | at most 271 | 1.00 at 2.5% | 0.044 at 5% |

For a sound validator the probability of passing S1–S4 together is 0.914 (simulated: the criteria
share cards; `oc.log`). If conditions are dropped (section 4), the n and k\* of S2–S4 are
recomputed by `oc.py` before the key is used. The validation **passes** only if S1–S4 all hold.

**Failure policy.** If any criterion fails, the result is published as it is, with the number that
failed it; there is no rescue language and no re-run. A new version of the engine is validated
only with a new pre-registration, a new key and new datasets; the v1 datasets then become
development data.

## 7. Secondary outcomes

Anchors R1–R3 (verdict against the allowed set, one by one, with the four fields); P4; the
dose–verdict curves; the per-level rates and the shared-donor intervals of section 5; run time and
memory per card.

2. When the network is open: select the backgrounds by section 3.1, download them, write
   `backgrounds.json`; commit.
3. Pilot, with the frozen code: `oracle.py` on the backgrounds → `pilot.json` (SESOI, key dose and

   flagship TMS case is run on v0.3, after the tag, so that the real case cannot have shaped the
   logic.

Nothing is excluded after step 3. A card the engine cannot process counts as an error (P2).

**For approval in this draft** (the agent's choices and findings):
- a. The pair-pool rule of section 3.1: the level thresholds (10% / 50% detection, mean 2), 4 pairs
  per level, the partial correlation beyond depth as the ranking (the metric's own co-detected
  correlation ranks the rarest genes at the low level), a negative control per pair with
  |partial r| < 0.02, one positive control per background from the high level.
- b. Δ\* per pair for the allowed sets and the card's direction, and the mean over a level's
  pairs for the key-dose rule; the SESOI of a level computed on B1 and used for B2 as well.
- c. The shared-donor interval of section 5 (donor-overlap covariance, Korn-Graubard).
- d. A finding of the dry runs (probe p14, simulated data, `validation/probes/p14_gate4_by_expression_level.log`):
  GATE 4 fails the valid metric where its response to the injected coupling is weak or absent. At
  the low level log-normalized Pearson does not see a coupling that acts through co-detection at
  all — its value on co-detected cells follows the cells' totals — so the engine's
  "NOT SUPPORTED — metric invalid" is right there, while Δ\* will be near zero; at the medium level
  a real but weak response fails z ≥ 3 in part of the datasets, a false FAIL, since GATE 4 has no
  power rule (GATE 5 has one). With the allowed sets as decided, a real effect the metric cannot
  see counts "NOT SUPPORTED — metric invalid" as an error. Proposed: where |Δ\*| < SESOI, NOT
  SUPPORTED is also correct for a real effect (the claimed change of the metric is below the
  SESOI, so the claim is not supported). Whether GATE 4 should get a power rule before the tag
  (with a dev-set probe and a journal entry), or the panel should measure the engine as it is, is
  the owner's decision; neither is implemented.
